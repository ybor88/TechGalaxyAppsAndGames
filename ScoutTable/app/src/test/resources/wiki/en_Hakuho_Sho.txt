{{nihongo|'''Hakuhō Shō'''|白鵬 翔||born 11 March 1985 as '''Mönkhbatyn Davaajargal''' ({{langx|mn|Мөнхбатын Даваажаргал}}; {{IPA|mn|mөnxpatʰin taw̜aːt͡ʃargaɮ|IPA}}|lead=yes}}) is a [[Mongols|Mongolian]]-born former professional [[sumo]] wrestler (''[[rikishi]]'') from [[Ulaanbaatar]], [[Mongolia]]. Making his debut in March 2001, he reached the top ''[[makuuchi]]'' division in May 2004. In May 2007 at age 22, he became the second native of Mongolia, the fourth non-Japanese, and 69th overall ''rikishi'' to be promoted to the highest rank in sumo, ''[[yokozuna]]''. With a record 45 total championships (''[[yūshō]]'') at the top ''makuuchi'' division, he is widely considered to be the greatest sumo wrestler of all time. He acquired [[Japanese citizenship]] in 2019.

In 2009, he broke the record for the most wins in a calendar year, winning 86 out of 90 bouts, and repeated this feat with the same record again in 2010 when he established the [[List of sumo record holders#Most consecutive wins|second longest winning streak]] in sumo history. He also holds the record for the most [[List of sumo record holders#Most top division championships|undefeated tournament championships]] at sixteen, which is eight more than any other sumo wrestler in history.

He was the only active ''yokozuna'' from 2010, following the retirement of his rival and fellow Mongolian [[Asashōryū Akinori|Asashōryū]], until 2012 with the promotion of fellow Mongolian [[Harumafuji Kōhei|Harumafuji]]. In March 2021, he became the only active ''yokozuna'' once again following the retirement of his rival and fellow Mongolian [[Kakuryū Rikisaburō|Kakuryū]] until the promotion of fellow Mongolian [[Terunofuji Haruo|Terunofuji]] four months later.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/03/443456bd50d5-breaking-news-sumo-association-approves-retirement-of-yokozuna-kakuryu.html|title=Sumo: Yokozuna Kakuryu retires after missing 5th straight tourney
|date=24 March 2021|work=Kyodo News|accessdate=24 March 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2021/07/19/sumo/terunofuji-yokozuna-promotion/|title=Terunofuji becomes sumo's 73rd yokozuna|work=[[The Japan Times]]|author=[[Kyodo News]]|date=21 July 2021|access-date=21 July 2021}}</ref>

In January 2015, he broke [[Taihō Kōki|Taihō]]'s long-standing record by winning his 33rd top division championship, the most in the history of sumo.<ref name="all time">{{cite news|url=https://blogs.wsj.com/japanrealtime/2015/01/23/sumo-yokozuna-hakuho-breaks-all-time-championship-win-record/|title=Sumo Yokozuna Hakuho Breaks All-Time Championship Win Record|last=Hongo|first=Jun|date=23 January 2015|work=[[The Wall Street Journal]]|access-date=23 January 2015}}</ref> He holds the records for [[List of sumo record holders#Most wins|most wins in the top division]], achieved in May 2016,<ref name="record">{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/05/09/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-posts-880th-makuuchi-victory/#.VzH3VE32aUk|title=Hakuho posts 880th makuuchi victory|date=9 May 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=10 May 2016}}</ref> and most career wins, achieved in July 2017.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2017/07/21/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-sets-time-wins-record-closes-nagoya-title/#.WXIsCE2Wzoo|title=Hakuho surpasses Kaio, sets all-time wins record|date=21 July 2017|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=21 July 2017}}</ref> He was the longest-serving ''yokozuna'' of all-time, having surpassed [[Haguroyama Masaji|Haguroyama]]'s record in 2019, and fought his 1000th bout as a ''yokozuna'' in July 2020. 

Hakuhō retired from professional sumo at the end of September 2021, closing out a 20-year career in the sport.<ref name=INTAI300921>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/09/472bbad81feb-breaking-news-sumo-wrestlings-record-setting-yokozuna-hakuho-retires.html|title=Sumo: Record-setting yokozuna Hakuho retires|date=30 September 2021|work=Kyodo News|access-date=30 September 2021}}</ref> Sumo commentator [[John Gunning (journalist)|John Gunning]] noted that Hakuhō left an unmatchable legacy,<ref name="legacy"/> while ''[[Washington Post]]'' columnist [[Stephen Stromberg]] called him "possibly the most accomplished figure in any sport, ever".<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2021/09/28/hakuho-sumo-wrestler-retire-japan-sports/|title=Opinion: The greatest figure in sports, maybe ever, just retired. You probably haven't heard of him.|last=Stromberg|first=Stephen|author-link=Stephen Stromberg |date=28 September 2021|newspaper=The Washington Post|access-date=30 September 2021}}</ref>

Hakuhō departed from the [[Japan Sumo Association]] in June 2025, after he was relieved from overseeing [[Miyagino stable|his stable]] in early 2024 following an investigation into assaults on the part of his wrestler [[Hokuseihō Osamu|Hokuseihō]].<ref name=kyodo_departure/>

==Early life and sumo background==
Like many of his countrymen in professional sumo, Hakuhō belongs to a family in the [[Mongolian wrestling]] tradition. His father [[Jigjidiin Mönkhbat]] won a silver medal in [[freestyle wrestling]] at the [[1968 Summer Olympics]],<ref name="student">{{cite news|author=Coskrey, Jason |title=Hakuho remains student of sumo despite success |newspaper=[[The Japan Times]]|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2009/04/19/sumo/hakuho-remains-student-of-sumo-despite-success/#.V7sDFGf2aUk|date=19 April 2009 |access-date=22 August 2016}}</ref> his country's first ever Olympic medal,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/kuchikomi/view/on-the-verge-of-breaking-sumos-all-time-record-hakuko-ponders-his-long-term-future|title=On the verge of breaking sumo's all-time record, Hakuho ponders his long-term future|date=7 December 2014|newspaper=Japan Today|access-date=22 August 2016}}</ref> and held the highest ranking in Mongolian wrestling, "Darkhan Avarga" (meaning "Undisputed Champion"), which is the Mongolian equivalent of ''yokozuna''. Davaajargal did not however have any formal training in Mongolian wrestling himself, as his father wished him to try other sports, and he concentrated on [[basketball]] as a child instead.<ref name=student/> However, at an early age he would be seen reading sumo magazines, and when his father asked him why he liked sumo so much, he responded by saying he wanted to be as big as a sumo wrestler one day. At that time he was considered below average in size.

He went to Japan in October 2000 when he was fifteen years old, invited by pioneering Mongolian wrestler [[Kyokushūzan Noboru|Kyokushūzan]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Grappling for the top spot|publisher=Trends in Japan|url=http://web-japan.org/trends/sports/spo060713.html|date=13 July 2006|access-date=12 June 2008}}</ref> Because he weighed only {{convert|62|kg|lb|abbr=on}}, no sumo training stable (''[[Heya (sumo)|heya]]'') was prepared to accept him. Hearing this, Kyokushūzan asked ''heya'' master [[Chikubayama Masakuni|Miyagino-''oyakata'']] to intercede, and Davaajargal was accepted to [[Miyagino stable]] on the last day of his two-month stay in Japan, 24 December 2000. He was given the ring name (''[[shikona]]'') ''Hakuhō'', with ''haku'' meaning "white" and ''hō'', meaning the Chinese mythological bird ''[[Peng (mythology)|Peng]]''. His ''shikona'' also emulates that of former ''yokozuna'' [[Taihō Kōki|Taihō]], whose rivalry with [[Kashiwado]] became known as the ''Hakuhō'' era.<ref>{{cite web|author=Buckton, Mark|title=Hakuho, and other foreign-born wrestlers, dominate the Autumn Basho |work=[[Japan Times]]|url=http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20070925mb.html|date=September 25, 2007|access-date=2008-06-12}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.nikkansports.com/sports/sumo/news/p-sp-tp3-20080506-356492.html|script-title=ja:大鵬の納谷氏が白鵬に「喝」入れる|language=ja|newspaper=[[Nikkan Sports]]|date=6 May 2008|access-date=12 June 2008}}</ref>

Hakuhō made his professional debut at the March tournament (''[[honbasho]]'') in [[Osaka]] in 2001<ref name="sumodb"/> despite having no previous wrestling experience. His weight increased steadily as he rose in the ranks reaching the second highest ''[[Professional sumo divisions#Jūryō|jūryō]]'' division in January 2004, and the top ''[[makuuchi]]'' division in May of the same year.<ref name="sumodb"/> In his very first top-division tournament, he scored twelve wins against three losses and was awarded a special prize (''[[sanshō (sumo)|sanshō]]'') for Fighting Spirit.<ref name="sumodb"/> He also enjoyed great success in subsequent tournaments, winning a gold star (''[[kinboshi]]'') for defeating ''yokozuna'' [[Asashōryū Akinori|Asashōryū]] in November 2004 while still at the lowest ''makuuchi'' rank of ''[[makuuchi#Maegashira|maegashira]]''.<ref name="sumodb"/> This tournament also saw him finish as runner-up for the first time.<ref name="sumodb"/> He achieved a rapid promotion to the rank of ''[[makuuchi#Komusubi|komusubi]]'' in January 2005 and ''[[makuuchi#Sekiwake|sekiwake]]'' only one tournament later.<ref name="sumodb"/> His progress was delayed by an injury which forced him to take leave (''zen-kyu'') from the [[Nagoya]] tournament in 2005.<ref name="sumodb"/>

His ''ōzeki'' promotion came in March 2006 after a 13–2 record, which included a [[yūshō#Playoffs|playoff]] for the championship (which he lost to Asashōryū) and also earned him two special prizes for Outstanding Performance and Technique.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20060327a1.html|title=Asashoryu beats Hakuho for 16th Cup|date=27 March 2006|work=The Japan Times|access-date=19 October 2009}}{{Dead link|date=July 2024 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> This gave him a three tournament record of 35 wins against ten losses. His promotion was confirmed just a few weeks after his twenty-first birthday, making him the fourth youngest wrestler to reach ''ōzeki'' in modern sumo history.<ref name="ozeki">{{cite news|date=22 May 2006|access-date=19 October 2009|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20060522a1.html|title=Mongolian Hakuho claims first Emperor's Cup|work=[[The Japan Times]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121004224015/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20060522a1.html|archive-date=4 October 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==''Ōzeki''==
At his first tournament as ''ōzeki'' in May 2006, with Asashōryū absent, Hakuhō won his first championship (''[[yūshō]]'') with a 14–1 record, defeating [[Miyabiyama Tetsushi|Miyabiyama]] in another playoff.<ref name=ozeki/> After another strong performance (13–2) in July, in which he finished as runner-up to Asashōryū and defeated him on the final day, Hakuhō was denied  promotion to ''yokozuna'', despite the [[Japan Sumo Association]]'s chairman [[Kitanoumi]] saying before the tournament that he would be considered if he posted 13 wins.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20060724a1.html|title=Hakuho beats Asa, denied promotion|date=24 July 2006|work=The Japan Times|access-date=19 October 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121004224003/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20060724a1.html|archive-date=4 October 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> Hakuhō told an interviewer that he had expected to be promoted.<ref name="nippon">{{cite web|url=https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g01215/|title="Yokozuna" Hakuhō: An Intimate Tale of a Sumō Grand Champion|last=Nagayama|first=Satoshi|work=nippon.com|date=20 December 2021|access-date=20 December 2021}}</ref> An uncharacteristically poor 8–7 showing in September put an end to any immediate promotion hopes.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20060925a1.html|title=Asa finishes strong|date=25 September 2006|work=The Japan injury sustained in training prevented him from participating in the November tournament,<ref>{{cite news|title=Hakuho out of Kyushu tourney|newspaper=The Japan Times Online|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2006/10/10/sumo/hakuho-out-of-kyushu-tourney|date=10 October 2006|access-date=20 December 2021}}</ref> putting him at risk for demotion (''[[kadoban (sumo)|kadoban]]'') in January 2007, when he scored a respectable ten wins on his return to the ring.<ref name="sumodb"/>

==Promotion to ''yokozuna''==
[[File:Hakuho Shiranui dohyo-iri 2012 Jan.JPG|thumb|right|250px|Hakuhō performs the Shiranui style ''[[dohyō-iri]]''.]]
In March 2007 Hakuhō won his second championship in [[Osaka]]<ref>{{cite news|title=Hakuho stuns Asa to win tourney|newspaper=The Japan Times Online|date=26 March 2007|access-date=27 May 2007|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070326b1.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121016072312/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070326b1.html|archive-date=16 October 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> and a third championship in the very next tournament in May, with a perfect 15–0 ([[Glossary of sumo terms#zenshō|zenshō]]) record.<ref>{{cite news|title=Undefeated Hakuho dominates Asashoryu|work=The Japan Times|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070528b1.html|date=28 May 2007|access-date=18 July 2007|archive-date=16 October 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121016072319/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070528b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Winning two consecutive championships satisfies the ''de facto'' requirements for promotion to the top rank in sumo. On the day following the tournament, the [[Yokozuna Deliberation Council]] unanimously recommended his promotion to ''yokozuna''<ref>{{cite news|agency=[[Kyodo News]]|title=Hakuho to receive promotion|newspaper=The Japan Times Online|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070529a2.html|date=29 May 2007|access-date=29 May 2007|archive-date=25 September 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120925192324/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20070529a2.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> which was formally announced by the [[Japan Sumo Association]] on 30 May 2007.<ref>{{cite news|title=Hakuho promoted to Yokozuna|publisher=Nihon Sumo Kyokai|url=http://www.sumo.or.jp/eng/kyokai/special/promotion/yokozuna_69th.html|date=30 May 2007|access-date=30 May 2007|archive-date=1 June 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070601162613/http://www.sumo.or.jp/eng/kyokai/special/promotion/yokozuna_69th.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> He had become ''yokozuna'' at the age of 22 years and two months – only Kitanoumi and [[Taihō Kōki|Taihō]] had been younger when they were promoted.<ref name="nippon"/> He performed his inaugural ring-entering ceremony (''[[dohyō-iri]]'') at the [[Meiji Shrine]] (in the lesser-used Shiranui style) on 1 June. He performed the ceremony at the [[Ryōgoku Kokugikan|Kokugikan]] during Kyokushūzan's retirement ceremony (''[[danpatsu-shiki]]'') on 2 June.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.daily.co.jp/general/2007/06/03/0000364519.shtml|script-title=ja:白鵬 国技館で初の土俵入り披露|language=ja|publisher=Daily Sports|date=3 June 2007|access-date=24 November 2008}} {{Dead link|date=February 2015}}</ref>

==''Yokozuna''==

===2007===
Hakuhō's first tournament as a ''yokozuna'' was in July 2007.<ref name="sumodb"/> His 25 match winning streak was brought to an end by [[Kotomitsuki Keiji|Kotomitsuki]] on the 10th day, and further losses to [[Kotoōshū Katsunori|Kotoōshū]] and [[Chiyotaikai Ryūji|Chiyotaikai]] put him out of contention for the title. He finished the tournament with an 11–4 record.<ref name="sumodb"/>

Hakuhō's first tournament championship as a ''yokozuna'' came in September 2007 with a 13–2 record, triumphing over Chiyotaikai on the last day.<ref name="sumodb"/> His second title as a ''yokozuna'', and fifth overall, came in the following tournament in November with a 12–3 score.<ref name="sumodb"/> He lost to Kotomitsuki on the final day but the championship had already been decided earlier in the day when his only challenger Chiyotaikai pulled out through injury.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2007/11/26/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-secures-fifth-title-after-chiyotaikai-withdraws/|title=Hakuho secures fifth title after Chiyotaikai withdraws|date=26 November 2007|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> His ''yokozuna'' rival Asashōryū missed both these tournaments through suspension.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2007/08/02/sumo/asashoryu-suspended-from-next-two-grand-tournaments/|title=Asashoryu suspended from next two grand tournaments|date=2 August 2007|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

[[File:Hakuho fight Jan08.jpg|thumb|left|150px|Hakuhō throws [[Dejima Takeharu|Dejima]] in the January 2008 tournament]]

===2008===
In the January 2008 tournament, he faced the returning Asashōryū on the final day with both wrestlers having a 13–1 score. In a bout lasting nearly a minute, Hakuhō defeated Asashōryū, winning his 6th championship with a 14–1 record. In the March 2008 tournament the two ''yokozuna'' met once again to decide the title and this time Asashōryū got his revenge, with Hakuhō finishing as runner-up.

In the May 2008 tournament, he won his first nine consecutive bouts.<ref name="sumodb"/> On the 10th day, however, he lost to [[Harumafuji Kōhei|Ama]] for the fourth time in their last five meetings, injuring his ankle in the process. Subsequent losses to Kotoōshū (the eventual winner of the tournament) and Kotomitsuki put him out of contention for the championship. He finished on 11–4, losing to Asashōryū on the final day in a match that sparked scandal after the two ''yokozuna'' nearly came to blows when Asashōryū gave Hakuhō an extra shove after the bout was over.<ref name=brouhaha>{{cite news|title=Asa, Hakuho warned for dohyo brouhaha|newspaper=Japan Times Online|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20080528a1.html|date=28 May 2008|access-date=29 May 2008|archive-date=31 May 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080531002033/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20080528a1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Both wrestlers were given a warning over their conduct by the [[Japan Sumo Association]].<ref name=brouhaha/>

In July 2008, with Asashōryū pulling out through injury he won his seventh championship without a serious challenge, securing victory by the 13th day: the first time this had been achieved since January 2005.<ref>{{cite news|title=Perfect Hakuho wraps up crown|work=[[The Japan Times]]|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20080728b1.html|date=28 July 2008|access-date=28 July 2008|archive-date=5 August 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080805205234/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20080728b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> He finished the tournament unbeaten; his second ''zenshō-yūshō''. Although he lost on day five of the September tournament, he still dominated all other opponents and secured another championship on the 14th day.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.iht.com/articles/ap/2008/09/27/sports/SUM-Japan-Roundup.php |title=Hakuho grabs eighth championship |work=[[International Herald Tribune]] |date=27 September 2008 |access-date=20 October 2008 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080930070845/http://www.iht.com/articles/ap/2008/09/27/sports/SUM-Japan-Roundup.php |archive-date=30 September 2008 }}</ref> He finished the tournament with a 14–1 record.

In the November tournament Hakuhō was once again the sole ''yokozuna'' participating. He lost his opening bout to Aminishiki and his 12th day bout to Ama. Both Hakuhō and Ama finished with a 13–2 record and the eventual play-off was won by Hakuhō, handing him his fourth ''yūshō'' of the season and ninth in total.<ref name="sumodb"/>

===2009===
[[File:Asashōryū-2.jpg|thumb|right|Hakuhō fights Asashōryū in an exhibition bout on 10 April 2009.]]
In the January 2009 tournament Hakuhō defeated Asashōryū on the final day in their first meeting since May, handing his fellow ''yokozuna'' his first defeat of the tournament and leaving both men with identical 14–1 records. Hakuhō was however beaten in the subsequent playoff.<ref>{{cite news|title=Asashoryu title at New Year sumo |work=International Herald Tribune |url=http://www.iht.com/articles/ap/2009/01/25/sports/SUM-Japan-Roundup.php |date=25 January 2009 |access-date=25 January 2009 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090129070105/http://iht.com/articles/ap/2009/01/25/sports/SUM-Japan-Roundup.php |archive-date=29 January 2009 }}</ref> Hakuhō defeated Asashōryū again in the March tournament, this time capping off an undefeated 15–0 championship, his third ''zenshō-yūshō'' and his tenth championship overall.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2009/03/30/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-finishes-perfect-in-osaka/|title=Hakuho finishes perfect in Osaka|date=30 March 2009|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

In May he extended his winning run to 33 regulation bouts, the best since Asashōryū's 35 in 2004, until he was defeated by Kotoōshū on Day 14. He recovered to beat Asashōryū on Day 15 to finish at 14–1, but he lost the playoff bout to [[Harumafuji]] who claimed his first championship.<ref name="sumodb"/>

In July he won his eleventh championship, finishing one win ahead of [[Kotoōshū]] with another 14–1 score.<ref name="sumodb"/> He almost pulled off his twelfth championship in the following September tournament. One win behind Asashōryū for most of the tournament after giving away his first ''[[kinboshi]]'' in a year (to [[Shōtenrō Taishi|Shōtenrō]]) he forced a playoff by beating his rival on the final day, but then lost in the succeeding playoff bout. This was a very similar scenario to his loss to Asashōryū in the preceding January tournament. Regardless of this loss, he still managed to become the first ''makuuchi'' wrestler ever to win fourteen or more bouts in five consecutive tournaments. He also became the first wrestler ever to lose three ''makuuchi'' playoffs in one year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sumodb.sumogames.de/Query.aspx?show_form=0&group_expand=on&group_by=rikishi&having=3&form1_yd=on&form1_m=on|title=Query result|publisher=Sumo Reference|access-date=3 October 2009}}</ref> After the tournament he was diagnosed with ligament damage in his left elbow; however surgery was not required.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20090930a1.html|title=Hakuho injures left elbow|date=30 September 2009|agency=Kyodo News|work=The Japan Times|access-date=30 September 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080805205234/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20090930a1.html|archive-date=5 August 2008|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 28 November, the fourteenth day of the Kyūshū basho, he clinched his twelfth tournament championship and broke Asashōryū's 2005 record for most bouts won in a calendar year, which had stood at 84.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20091129b1.html|title=Hakuho earns 12th Emperor's Cup|date=29 November 2009|work=The Japan Times|access-date=30 November 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091203180413/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20091129b1.html|archive-date=3 December 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref> He defeated Asashōryū the following day to secure his fourth career ''zenshō-yūshō'' and set his 2009 record total at 86 wins.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20091130b1.html|title=Hakuho stays undefeated|date=30 November 2009|work=The Japan Times|access-date=30 November 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100119153325/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20091130b1.html|archive-date=19 January 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> This was also his fourteenth consecutive ''yūshō'' or ''jun-yūshō'' (winner or runner-up) performance, another record.<ref name="sumodb"/>

===2010===
In the opening tournament of the year Hakuhō's 30 bout winning streak was ended by [[Baruto Kaito|Baruto]] on Day 7, and he suffered consecutive losses to ''ozeki'' Harumafuji and [[Kaiō Hiroyuki|Kaiō]] on Days 12 and 13 to concede the title to Asashōryū by Day 14. He gained some consolation by beating his ''yokozuna'' rival for the seventh straight time in regulation bouts on the final day to finish the tournament on 12–3.<ref name="sumodb"/>

Hakuhō expressed his shock at the retirement of Asashōryū in February, following allegations his fellow ''yokozuna'' had assaulted a man in a drunken brawl outside a nightclub during the previous tournament. Fighting back tears he said, "I don't want to believe it. I was honoured to wrestle in the same era as him."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://english.kyodonews.jp/?photoid=43389 |title=Scandal-tainted champion Asashoryu calls it quit |agency=[[Kyodo News]] |date=4 February 2010 |access-date=6 February 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110722112033/http://english.kyodonews.jp/?photoid=43389 |archive-date=22 July 2011 }}</ref> After getting regularly beaten by Asashōryū earlier in his career, Hakuhō came to completely dominate him, winning all of their last seven regulation matches (excluding two tournament-playoff defeats) and finishing with a 14–13 record over his greatest rival.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Sumo-through-Wrestlers-Eyes-ebook/dp/B006C1I5K8/ref=sr_1_1?s=digital-text&ie=UTF8&qid=1322321867&sr=1-1|title=Amazon.com: Sumo Through The Wrestlers' Eyes eBook: Chris Gould: Kindle Store|work=amazon.com}}</ref>

He won the Osaka tournament in March with a perfect 15–0 record, his fifth undefeated score and thirteenth championship overall. After his victory he spoke of the extra pressure now that he was sumo's lone yokozuna and his relief at the win.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100330a2.html|title=Hakuho admits relief at Osaka win|date=30 March 2010|work=The Japan Times|access-date=29 March 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100429141900/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100330a2.html|archive-date=29 April 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Hakuhō wrapped up his fourteenth championship in May by Day 13 (his earliest ''yūshō'' win since July 2008) and went on to record his sixth ''zenshō-yūshō'', the first time he has achieved this in consecutive tournaments. With the win, he equalled the number of ''yūshō'' won by ''yokozuna'' [[Wajima Hiroshi|Wajima]], and to commemorate this he switched to wearing Wajima's trademark gold coloured ''[[mawashi]]''.<ref>{{cite news|title=Sumo: Hakuho gets 2nd straight perfect win at summer sumo |url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20100523p2g00m0sp048000c.html |publisher=[[Mainichi Daily News]] |access-date=23 May 2010 }}{{dead link|date=June 2016|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>

In July 2010 a special committee reviewing the extent of illegal gambling within sumo revealed that Hakuhō had bet several tens of thousands of yen on ''[[hanafuda]]'' Japanese card games with his fellow wrestlers twice a year or so. However, the committee said that he would not be punished as it was not considered a serious offence.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/mdnnews/news/20100703p2g00m0dm004000c.html|title=Sumo: gambling survey finds no new figures|date=3 July 2010|publisher=The Mainichi Daily News|access-date=3 July 2010}} {{Dead link|date=January 2015}}</ref> He nonetheless appeared along with nearly 80 other wrestlers at a press conference and apologised to sumo fans for his actions.<ref>{{cite news|title=Nearly 80 wrestlers apologize for gambling scandal at press conference|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/mdnnews/news/20100705p2a00m0na016000c.html|publisher=Mainichi Daily News|access-date=14 July 2010|date=5 July 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://archive.today/20100712033754/http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/national/archive/news/2010/07/05/20100705p2a00m0na016000c.html|archive-date=12 July 2010}}</ref> On the 14th day of the Nagoya tournament he won his 46th consecutive bout, surpassing Taihō's 45, behind only [[Chiyonofuji Mitsugu|Chiyonofuji]]'s 53 and [[Futabayama Sadaji|Futabayama]]'s 69 as the longest winning run since the beginning of the [[Shōwa era]]. He clinched his fifteenth ''yūshō'' on the same day, and on the final day he secured his third consecutive 15–0 record, the first wrestler ever to achieve this.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sumo: Hakuho 1st to three-peat with perfect 15–0 records|url=http://newsystocks.com/news/3610293|publisher=Kyodo News International|access-date=25 July 2010|date=25 July 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110714175021/http://newsystocks.com/news/3610293|archive-date=14 July 2011}}</ref> However, he did not receive the [[Glossary of sumo terms#tennō-hai|Emperor's Cup]] or any other trophy, as the Sumo Association decided to withdraw them in response to the gambling scandal.<ref name=hollow>{{cite news|title=Hollow victory for sumo's grand champion |url=http://www.dailytimes.com.pk/default.asp?page=2010\07\26\story_26-7-2010_pg2_9 |newspaper=[[Daily Times (Pakistan)|Daily Times]] |access-date=26 July 2010 |date=26 July 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100613231132/http://www.dailytimes.com.pk/default.asp?page=2010%5C06%5C09%5Cstory_9-6-2010_pg12_2 |archive-date=13 June 2010 }}</ref> Hakuhō commented, "I hope we will not have a tournament like this ever again."<ref name=hollow/>

On Day 6 of the Aki ''basho'' in September he equalled the 53-bout [[List of sumo record holders#Most wins|winning streak]] of Chiyonofuji with a win over [[Kotoshōgiku]],<ref>{{cite news|last=Hueston|first=Dave|title=Hakuho breezes past another foe|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100918b1.html|work=The Japan Times|access-date=18 September 2010|date=18 September 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100923213424/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100918b1.html|archive-date=23 September 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> and surpassed it the following day by pushing out [[Kisenosato]] in front of the first sell-out crowd of the tournament so far.<ref name=postwar>{{cite news|title=Hakuho owns longest postwar win streak|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100919b1.html|author=Dave Hueston|agency=Kyodo News|work=The Japan Times|access-date=25 September 2010|date=25 September 2010|archive-date=29 October 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101029074747/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100919b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> He said afterwards that he felt "I really repaid my debt of gratitude" to the former Chiyonofuji.<ref name=postwar/> He secured his fourth consecutive ''yūshō'' on the fourteenth day when rank-and-filers [[Yoshikaze]] and [[Takekaze]] suffered defeats, and he moved to 14–0 (and 61 consecutive wins) by beating Kotoōshū. Asked about Futabayama's record of 69 wins, set in the two tournament a year era from 1936 to 1939, he responded, "It is truly amazing that he was able to continue winning for almost three years."<ref name=hurdle>{{cite news|title=Sumo: Hakuho reaches last hurdle toward history|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/mdnnews/sports/news/20100925p2g00m0sp059000c.html|publisher=Mainichi Daily News|date=25 September 2010}} {{Dead link|date=January 2015}}</ref> Former Sumo Association [[Kitanoumi]] estimated that Hakuhō had a possibility of "about 80 percent" of breaking the record, which he would achieve on the eighth day of the November tournament.<ref name=hurdle/> Hakuhō wrapped up the Aki ''basho'' by defeating Harumafuji to achieve his fourth perfect record in a row. This was also his eighth ''zenshō-yūshō'' overall, equalling the record held jointly Futabayama and Taihō.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hueston|first=Dave|title=Hakuho finishes flawless again|date=27 September 2010|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100927b1.html|work=The Japan Times|access-date=27 September 2010|archive-date=30 September 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100930202952/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20100927b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On the first day of the November 2010 tournament, Hakuhō defeated [[Tochinoshin]], tying the consecutive wins record of [[Tanikaze Kajinosuke|Tanikaze]] with 63.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/sports/view/hakuho-kicks-off-bid-to-make-sumo-history-in-kyushu|title=Hakuho kicks off bid to make sumo history in Kyushu|agency=Kyodo News|work=Japan Today|date=14 November 2010|access-date=15 November 2010}} {{Dead link|date=January 2015}}</ref> However, on the following day his run was finally brought to an end when he was defeated by Kisenosato.<ref>{{cite news|title=Sumo: Yokozuna Hakuho's winning streak ends at 63 |url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20101115p2g00m0sp073000c.html |publisher=Mainichi Daily News |date=15 November 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101119012811/http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20101115p2g00m0sp073000c.html |archive-date=19 November 2010 }}</ref> This was only the fifth time in his ''yokozuna'' career that Hakuhō has been defeated by a ''maegashira'', and Kisenosato is the first to earn more than one ''[[kinboshi]]'' from him, having previously upset him in September 2008. However, Hakuhō won all his remaining bouts and defeated ''maegashira'' [[Toyonoshima]] in a playoff to win the championship. He finished the year on 86 wins in regulation matches, equalling the record he set in 2009. At a press conference following his victory, he revealed that having his winning run halted before breaking Futabayama's record affected him so badly that he considered withdrawing from the tournament.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20101130a1.html|work=The Japan Times|title=Hakuho almost quit basho after loss|date=30 November 2010|access-date=2 December 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101204073742/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20101130a1.html|archive-date=4 December 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 21 December he was awarded the [[Japan Professional Sports Grand Prize]], receiving the Prime Minister's Trophy from [[Naoto Kan]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Yokozuna Hakuho wins Japan Professional Sports Grand Prize |url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20101222p2a00m0na005000c.html |publisher=Mainichi Daily News |date=22 December 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101223190522/http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20101222p2a00m0na005000c.html |archive-date=23 December 2010 }}</ref>

===2011===
In the January 2011 tournament in Tokyo he was surprisingly beaten by Kisenosato for the second time in a row but he secured his eighteenth championship on the fourteenth day. In doing so Hakuhō became only the third man after Taihō and Asashōryū to win six consecutive tournaments.<ref name=note>{{cite news|title=Hakuho finishes New Year tournament on winning note|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110124b1.html|work=The Japan Times|agency=Kyodo News|access-date=17 February 2011|date=24 January 2011|archive-date=25 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110125233731/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110124b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

During the May "technical examination tournament" Hakuhō notched up his 500th win in the top division, with a victory over [[Kitataiki]] on Day 5.<ref name=notches>{{cite web|title=Hakuho notches 500th career win in makuuchi|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/sports/view/hakuho-notches-500th-career-win-in-makuuchi|work=[[Japan Today]]|access-date=13 May 2011|date=12 May 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110514050733/http://www.japantoday.com/category/sports/view/hakuho-notches-500th-career-win-in-makuuchi|archive-date=14 May 2011}}</ref> He achieved this total with the loss of just 99 top division bouts since his debut in May 2004 – a winning percentage of 83%.<ref name=notches/> He was defeated by Harumafuji on Day 13 but went on to win his seventh straight championship, equalling Asashōryū's record, despite losing to Kaiō on the final day.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hueston|first=Dave|title=Hakuho wins test meet despite final-day loss|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110523b1.html|work=The Japan Times|access-date=23 May 2011|date=23 May 2011|archive-date=25 May 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110525054449/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110523b1.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Hakuhō was defeated on the eleventh day of the July 2011 tournament by ''sekiwake'' [[Kotoshōgiku]] and his quest for a record eighth straight ''yūshō'' ended on Day 14 when he was beaten by Harumafuji to drop two wins behind.<ref>{{cite news|title=Harumafuji clinches second Emperor's Cup of career |url=http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110724b1.html |work=The Japan Times |access-date=July 25, 2011 |date=July 24, 2011 |archive-date=July 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110724224413/http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20110724b1.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> He also lost his final day match to Baruto to finish on 12–3, his poorest result since January 2010. Nevertheless, it was still enough for runner-up honours, his twelfth.<ref name="sumodb"/>

He was defeated by Kisenosato for the third time in five meetings on Day 12 of the September tournament and then lost to Kotoshōgiku for the second time in a row the following day. However he rallied to beat Baruto on Day 14 and then Harumafuji on the final day to clinch his twentieth tournament championship.<ref name=autumn>{{cite web|title=Sumo: Hakuho wins 20th career title at autumn sumo|url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20110925p2g00m0sp033000c.html|publisher=[[Mainichi Daily News]]|date=25 September 2011|archive-date=25 May 2024|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://archive.today/20240525122329/https://www.webcitation.org/61yJ5LEID?url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20110925p2g00m0sp033000c.html}}</ref> On 25 November 2011, he won his 21st tournament title in Fukuoka, moving to 13–0 with none of his rivals scoring better than 10–3.<ref>{{cite web|title=Yokozuna Hakuho wins Emperor's Cup|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20111126b1.html|work=The Japan Times|access-date=28 November 2011|date=26 November 2011}}</ref> He finished the tournament on 14–1, his only loss coming to Baruto on the final day.<ref>{{cite news|title=Kisenosato promoted despite final loss|agency=Kyodo News|work=The Japan Times|date=28 November 2011|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20111128b1.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111129114517/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/text/ss20111128b1.html|archive-date=29 November 2011|access-date=28 November 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===2012===
Hakuhō finished second to [[Baruto Kaito|Baruto]] in the opening tournament of 2012, losing to [[Kakuryū Rikisaburō|Kakuryū]], Harumafuji and Kotoōshū. He did however maintain his record of finishing runner-up or better in his last 26 tournaments. In the March ''basho'', Hakuhō won his twenty-second ''yūshō'' after beating Kakuryū in a playoff, finishing with a 13–2 record.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://japantoday.com/category/sports/hakuho-beats-kakuryu-to-clinch-22nd-title|title=Hakuho beats Kakuryu to clinch 22nd title|date=25 March 2012|work=Japan Today|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> Hakuhō's only losses came to Kakuryū on the 9th day and [[Kisenosato]] on the 13th day. Kakuryū had entered the final day of the tournament one match ahead of the ''yokozuna'' but lost to [[Gōeidō Gōtarō|Gōeidō]], and Hakuhō beat Baruto to force a playoff. This marked the first time a wrestler had come from one win behind to claim the ''yūshō'' on the final day since Asashōryū defeated [[Hokutōriki]] in a playoff in May 2004.<ref name=rallies>{{cite news|title=Sumo: Hakuho rallies to win 22nd career title at spring sumo|url=http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20120326p2g00m0sp027000c.html|publisher=Mainichi Daily News|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120326172324/http://mdn.mainichi.jp/mdnnews/sports/news/20120326p2g00m0sp027000c.html|archive-date=26 March 2012|url-status=dead|date=26 March 2012}}</ref> With this victory Hakuhō drew level with Takanohana in fifth place on the all–time list of most top division tournament championships.<ref name=rallies/>

In the May 2012 tournament Hakuhō fractured his left index finger in an opening day loss to Aminishiki, and he dropped further matches to [[Toyohibiki]], Gōeidō and [[Toyonoshima]] on Days 7, 8 and 9 to stand at only 5–4 after nine days.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sumo: Hakuho has fractured finger, could pull out of summer basho|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/english/newsselect/news/20120515p2g00m0sp007000c.html|publisher=Mainichi Weekly|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120710103656/http://mainichi.jp/english/english/newsselect/news/20120515p2g00m0sp007000c.html|archive-date=10 July 2012|url-status=dead|date=15 May 2012}}</ref> However he then won five bouts in a row and was even in with an outside chance of claiming the ''yūshō'' until it was announced that Kotoōshū was withdrawing on the final day and giving [[Tochiōzan]] an automatic twelfth win. Hakuhō's defeat by Harumafuji on Day 15 meant he finished on 10–5, his worst ever score as a ''yokozuna'' and the first time since his debut at the rank, 29 tournaments ago, that he failed to be at least the runner–up.<ref name="sumodb"/>

After losing to Harumafuji on the last day in both the July and September tournaments (as well as ''maegashira'' Tochiōzan in September) and seeing his fellow Mongolian claim the ''yūshō'' in both and earn promotion to ''yokozuna'' himself,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2012/09/24/sumo/harumafuji-wins-title-set-for-yokozuna-rank/|title=Harumafuji wins title, set for yokozuna rank|date=24 September 2012|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> Hakuhō came back to win his 23rd championship in November, losing only to Kotoōshū on Day 11. He also finished as the wrestler with the most wins in the calendar year for the sixth consecutive time, a record. His victory was also his sixth straight Kyushu tournament triumph, the best run since [[Chiyonofuji]]'s record eight in a row from 1981 to 1988.

===2013===
Hakuhō finished joint runner up on 12–3 in the opening tournament of 2013, giving up a ''[[kinboshi]]'' to [[Myōgiryū Yasunari|Myōgiryū]] on Day 3 and also losing to Kotoōshū and Harumafuji.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/01/28/sumo/harumafuji-claims-perfect-record/#.UQmdIvL8mSo|title=Harumafuji claims perfect record|date=28 January 2013|work=The Japan Times|access-date=30 January 2013}}</ref> However he captured his 24th title in the Haru ''basho'' in March, remaining undefeated for the whole fifteen days.<ref name="hakuhoperfect">{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/03/25/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-finishes-with-perfect-record/|title=Hakuho finishes with perfect record|date=25 March 2013|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> This was his first ''zenshō-yūshō'' since his record winning streak of 2010 and also the ninth of his career, breaking the all-time record he had shared with [[Taiho Koki|Taihō]] and [[Futabayama]].<ref name="hakuhoperfect"/> It also drew him level with [[Kitanoumi]] in fourth place on the list of most career championships,<ref name="hakuhoperfect"/> behind only Asashōryū with 25, Chiyonofuji with 31 and Taihō with 32. Hakuhō also recorded his 650th win in the top division in this tournament, moving him into eighth place all-time. In the May tournament he won his 25th championship, equaling Asashōryū's mark. By again recording an undefeated tournament record, his final two victories coming over Kisenosato and Harumafuji, he increased his winning streak to 30 bouts.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/05/27/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-captures-title-as-kisenosato-loses-way/|title=Hakuho captures title as Kisenosato loses way|date=27 May 2013|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

Hakuhō extended his streak to 43 bouts by winning his first 13 matches in the Nagoya tournament in July, assuring himself of his 26th tournament victory with two days to spare.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/07/19/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-clinches-title/|title=Hakuho clinches title|date=19 July 2013|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He was finally defeated by Kisenosato on Day 14,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/07/20/sumo/basho-reports/kisenosato-puts-an-end-to-hakuho-win-streak/|title=Kisenosato puts an end to Hakuho win streak|date=20 July 2013|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> and also lost to Harumafuji on the final day. He won his fourth tournament in a row, and the 27th of his career, in the Aki ''basho'' in September, losing only one bout to [[Gōeidō Gōtarō|Gōeidō]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/09/28/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-tops-kisenosato-for-27th-crown-of-career/|title=Hakuho tops Kisenosato for 27th crown of career|work=Japan Times|date=28 September 2013|publisher=28 September 2013|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> In November he went into a final day showdown with his fellow ''yokozuna'' Harumafuji, with both men tied on 13–1. Harumafuji won this bout to claim his sixth championship, with Hakuhō having to settle for his 17th second-place result.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2013/11/24/sumo/basho-reports/harumafuji-claims-title-with-win-over-hakuho/|title=Harumafuji claims title with win over Hakuho|date=24 November 2013|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He finished the year with 82 wins out of 90 bouts, a record that has only been bettered by himself (twice) and Asashōryū.

===2014===
[[File:Hakuhō vs Gōeidō May 2014.jpg|thumb|right|Hakuhō (left) about to begin his match with [[Gōeidō]], May 2014]]
Hakuhō won the first tournament of 2014. After going undefeated in his first 14 matches, Hakuhō lost to [[Kakuryū Rikisaburō|Kakuryū]] on the final day. As Kakuryū had a 14–1 record, this prompted the two to again meet in a tie-breaking bout. After losing 20 minutes prior, Hakuhō won his twenty-eighth ''yūshō'' by defeating Kakuryū in this tie breaker.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/01/26/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-beats-kakuryu-in-playoff-to-capture-28th-title/|title=Hakuho beats Kakuryu in playoff to capture 28th title|date=26 January 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

In the March tournament, Hakuhō was bested by Kakuryū and finished runner-up, tied with [[Gōeidō Gōtarō|Gōeidō]] at 12–3.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/03/23/sumo/basho-reports/mongolian-kakuryu-captures-first-emperors-cup-with-14-1-record/|title=Mongolian Kakuryu captures first Emperor's Cup with 14-1 record|date=23 March 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He returned in May to win his twenty-ninth ''yūshō'' with a record of 14–1, only losing once to Gōeidō.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/05/25/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-beats-rival-collect-29th-title/|title=Hakuho beats rival Harumafuji to collect 29th title|date=25 May 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He then won his thirtieth ''yūshō'' with a 13–2 record in July, becoming only the third man in history to have 30 or more top division championships.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/07/27/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-captures-30th-emperors-cup/|title=Hakuho captures 30th Emperor's Cup|date=27 July 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

On Day 14 of the September tournament, he defeated the up-and-coming [[Ichinojō Takashi|Ichinojō]], who he was tied with at 12–1.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/09/27/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-grabs-sole-lead-pivotal-win-ichinojo/|title=Hakuho grabs sole lead with pivotal win over Ichinojo|date=27 September 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> The following day, Hakuhō clinched the championship, winning his third tournament in a row, and the 31st of his career, having lost only one bout, to Gōeidō.<ref name="dominating">{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/09/28/sumo/basho-reports/dominating-hakuho-wins-31st-career-title/|title=Dominating Hakuho wins 31st career title|date=28 September 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> It also drew him level with Chiyonofuji in second place on the list of most career championships.<ref name="dominating"/>

In the next tournament in Fukuoka, he continued his dominance, again losing only one bout (against [[Takayasu Akira|Takayasu]] on day six<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/11/14/sumo/basho-reports/kakuryu-takes-sole-lead/|title=Kakuryu takes sole lead|date=14 November 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>) and taking the championship to tie him with [[Taihō Kōki|Taihō]] for the most championships in sumo history, defeating fellow ''yokozuna'' Kakuryū with a ''yorikiri'' push-out, for a 14–1 record and his 32nd Emperor's Cup.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2014/11/23/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-ties-taihos-mark-32nd-title/|title=Hakuho ties Taiho's mark with 32nd title|date=23 November 2014|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> Hakuhō finally was able to fulfill a promise he had made during a visit to Taihō just two days before his death in January 2013 and equal the record,<ref name=promise>{{cite news|url=http://ajw.asahi.com/article/behind_news/sports/AJ201411240022|author=Noriyasu Nukui|title=SUMO/ Promise made to fabled Taiho drove Hakuho to record-tying 32nd championship|date=24 November 2014|newspaper=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=3 January 2015|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150104014216/http://ajw.asahi.com/article/behind_news/sports/AJ201411240022|archive-date=4 January 2015}}</ref> which had stood for over 40 years. After an emotional presentation ceremony Hakuhō remarked, "I could achieve this accomplishment because the soul of the Japanese and the god of sumo gave a mandate to my efforts".<ref name="promise"/>

===2015===
Hakuhō broke Taihō's record in the opening tournament of 2015, clinching the championship on the 13th day by defeating [[Kisenosato]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/01/23/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-bests-legendary-taihos-record-with-33rd-carreer-championship/|title=Hakuho bests legendary Taiho's record with 33rd career championship|date=23 January 2015|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> and maintaining a perfect score into the final days. Hakuhō finished the tournament with a perfect score of 15–0 while all contenders had at least four losses.<ref name="all time"/> [[Sumo Association]] chairman [[Kitanoumi]] commented, "Nobody can touch Hakuho... I’d like to see him go for 40 titles. If he keeps going the way he is, that’s a possibility."<ref>{{cite news|date=25 January 2015 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2015/jan/25/hakuho-rewrites-sumo-history-with-record-breaking-33rd-championship|title=Hakuho rewrites sumo history with record-breaking 33rd championship|last=Graham|first=Bryan Armen|work=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=27 January 2015}}</ref> He was however criticized for turning up an hour late to his press conference the next day, having reportedly been out drinking until 7am celebrating his victory.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.highsnobiety.com/2015/09/09/japan-sumo-crisis/|title=Why Japan's Sumo Culture is in Crisis|last=Edwards|first=Mark|date=9 September 2015|publisher=Hignsnobiety|access-date=6 June 2016}}</ref>

In Osaka in March Hakuhō won his sixth consecutive championship, a feat only achieved four times previously (twice by Taihō, once by Asashōryū and once by himself). His 36 bout winning streak was brought to an end by [[Terunofuji]] on Day 13,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/03/22/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-captures-34th-emperors-cup/#.VRFXzJ3D-70|title=Hakuho captures 34th Emperor's Cup|date=22 March 2015|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=24 March 2015}}</ref> but he won all his other matches to finish one ahead of Terunofuji on 14–1. During this tournament he also overtook Chiyonofuji to move into second place on the all-time list of most wins in the top division, behind only Kaiō. In May he lost on the opening day of a tournament for the first time in three years, to [[Ichinojo]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/05/10/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-suffers-loss-on-tourneys-first-day/|title=Hakuho suffers loss on tourney's first day|date=10 May 2015|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He ended up finishing runner-up to Terunofuji on 11–4, thus ending his hopes of equalling his and Asashōryū's record of seven straight championships.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/05/24/sumo/basho-reports/sekiwake-terunofuji-wins-title-after-hakuho-stumbles/|title=Sekiwake Terunofuji wins title after Hakuho stumbles|date=24 May 2015|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> However, he won his 35th title in Nagoya in July, finishing two wins ahead of the rest of the field on 14–1.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/07/26/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-triumphs-claim-35th-career-title/|title=Hakuho triumphs to claim 35th career title|date=26 July 2015|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref>

In September Hakuhō lost to [[Okinoumi]] and [[Yoshikaze]] on the first two days,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/09/14/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-loses-second-straight-bout-nightmare-start-autumn-basho/|title=Hakuho loses second straight bout in nightmare start to Autumn Basho|date=14 September 2015|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> and then announced that he would miss the rest of the tournament owing to an injury which was diagnosed as [[tendinitis]] in the left [[quadriceps]]. After withdrawing from a tournament for the first time since 2006 Hakuhō said "I couldn’t get any power. I apologize to everyone. I can’t step into the ring with half-hearted feelings".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://the-japan-news.com/news/article/0002425257|title=Injured Hakuho withdraws|author=staff |work=[[Yomiuri Shimbun]]}}</ref> His withdrawal brought to an end his run of 722 consecutive matchdays competing as a ''yokozuna'' and 51 consecutive tournaments with a score of 10–5 or better, both records.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/09/15/sumo/basho-reports/yokozuna-hakuho-to-likely-withdraw-from-autumn-basho/#.VfjMC_lVikp|title=Yokozuna Hakuho withdraws from Autumn Basho|date=15 September 2015|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=16 September 2015}}</ref> On his return in November he appeared to be in dominant form and won his first twelve matches, but defeats in the last three days to Harumafuji, Terunofuji and Kakuryū saw him end the tournament in a three-way tie for second place.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2015/11/22/sumo/basho-reports/harumafuji-ends-two-year-title-drought-2/|title=Harumafuji ends two-year title drought|date=22 November 2015|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> His most unusual win came on day 10 when he used the rarely-seen ''[[nekodamashi]]'' ("cat trick") technique to defeat [[Tochiōzan]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.jp/photos/2015/11/384720.html|title=Hakuho continues march toward title at Kyushu sumo tournament|work=kyodonews.jp}}</ref>

===2016===
In January 2016 Hakuhō won his first ten matches but then lost to Kotoshōgiku, the eventual winner of the tournament, on Day 11.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/01/20/sumo/basho-reports/kotoshogiku-gets-yokozuna-hakuho-claim-sole-lead/|title=Kotoshogiku gets by yokozuna Hakuho to claim sole lead|date=20 January 2016|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> He also lost his last two bouts to Kisenosato and Harumafuji to end with a 12–3 record and a tie for second place.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/01/24/sumo/basho-reports/kotoshogiku-ends-japans-decade-long-wait-emperors-cup-winner/|title=Kotoshogiku ends Japan's decade-long wait for Emperor's Cup winner|date=24 January 2016|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> This result meant he had gone three tournaments without winning the championship for the first time since 2012. In March he lost to [[Takarafuji]] on the first day,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/03/13/sumo/basho-reports/kotoshogiku-starts-strong-hakuho-falters-opening-day/|title=Kotoshogiku starts strong as Hakuho falters on opening day|date=13 March 2016|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> but won his remaining fourteen matches to take his 36th championship. His win was poorly received as he employed a ''henka'' (sidestep at the initial charge) to defeat Harumafuji on the final day and clinch the title.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/sports/view/hakuho-wins-36th-career-title|title=Hakuho wins 36th career title|date=27 March 2016|newspaper=Japan Today}}{{dead link|date=October 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> Speaking after the match Hakuhō said that he had never planned to win with a ''henka'' "and I feel really bad about that."<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/03/27/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-wins-title-frowned-upon-move/#.Vvp52E32aUk|title=Hakuho wins title with frowned-upon move|date=27 March 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=29 March 2016}}</ref>

On the opening day of the May tournament in Tokyo he equalled Kaiō's [[List of sumo record holders#most top division wins|record of 879 top division wins]], and surpassed it the next day by gaining revenge over Takarafuji who had defeated him in the previous tournament.<ref name="record"/> He secured his 37th championship on the fourteenth day when his only challenger Kisenosato fell to his second loss, and he rounded off the tournament by defeating Kakuryū with a rare backward pivot throw (''utchari'') to ensure a 29th consecutive victory and a perfect 15–0 record.<ref name="perfect">{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/05/22/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-bags-perfect-mark/#.V0LzUmf2aM8|title=Hakuho bags perfect mark|date=22 May 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=23 May 2016}}</ref> His winning streak of 33 bouts was brought to an end on the 5th day of the July 2016 tournament when he lost once again to Takarafuji.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/07/14/sumo/basho-reports/yokozuna-hakuhos-33-match-win-streak-ends-kisenosato-handed-first-defeat-nagoya/#.V4ejnE32Yy8|title=Yokozuna Hakuho's 33-match win streak ends; Kisenosato handed first defeat in Nagoya|date=14 July 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=14 July 2016}}</ref> He finished the tournament with a mediocre 10–5 record, hampered by an injury to his right big toe.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://kyodonews.net/news/2016/09/05/78329|title=Sumo: Hakuho struggling to be fit for Autumn tourney|date=5 September 2016|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=7 September 2016|archive-date=6 September 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160906160726/http://kyodonews.net/news/2016/09/05/78329|url-status=dead}}</ref> He struggled to recover from his injuries and on 8 September he announced that he would miss the whole of the upcoming tournament explaining "I’m sorry to the fans. I want to work on healing in time for the autumn regional tour and the Kyushu Basho".<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/09/08/sumo/basho-reports/hakuho-pulls-basho-due-injuries/|title=Hakuho pulls out of basho due to injuries|date=8 September 2016|newspaper=Japan Times Online}}</ref>

After undergoing surgery on his toe injury Hakuhō returned to action at the November tournament. On the third day he became only the third wrestler, after Chiyonofuji and Kaiō, to reach 1000 career wins.<ref name="career">{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/11/15/sumo/basho-reports/legend-hakuho-earns-1000th-career-victory/#.WCsQPU0zUy8|title=Legend Hakuhō earns 1,000th career victory|date=15 November 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=15 November 2016}}</ref> Afterwards he admitted to nerves having fallen short of his target with a 10–5 record in July and then having to sit out the September tournament, and expressed relief that he was "finally able to do it."<ref name="career"/> Hakuhō went on to get an 11–4 record in November.<ref name="sumodb"/>

===2017===
In the opening tournament of 2017 Hakuhō lost on Day 8 to ''maegashira'' [[Arawashi Tsuyoshi|Arawashi]] (a rare example of him losing to a wrestler he had never fought before) and ''komusubi'' [[Takayasu Akira|Takayasu]] on Day 9.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2017/01/15/sumo/basho-reports/loss-hakuho-puts-kisenosato-control/|title=Loss for Hakuho puts Kisenosato in control|date=15 January 2017|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2017/01/16/sumo/basho-reports/kisenosato-hakuho-lose-day-shocks/|title=Kisenosato, Hakuho both lose on day of shocks|date=16 January 2017|work=Japan Times|access-date=28 September 2021}}</ref> After being defeated on the last two days by [[Takanoiwa]] and tournament winner [[Kisenosato]], he finished in a share for third place. This marked the first time in Hakuhō's ''yokozuna'' career that he had gone four tournaments without winning the championship.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20170122/p2g/00m/0sp/053000c|title=Sumo: New Year champ Kisenosato beats Hakuho, set for yokozuna|date=22 January 2017|newspaper=The Mainichi|access-date=23 January 2017|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123081146/http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20170122/p2g/00m/0sp/053000c|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref> He pulled out of the March tournament on the fifth day because of toe and thigh injuries.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20170316/p2g/00m/0sp/058000c|title=Sumo: Kisenosato keeps winning run going at Spring sumo|date=16 March 2017|newspaper=The Mainichi|access-date=16 March 2017|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170317143105/http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20170316/p2g/00m/0sp/058000c|archive-date=17 March 2017}}</ref>

The May 2017 tournament saw Hakuhō ranked as a ''yokozuna'' on the ''[[banzuke]]'' for the 59th time, putting him level with Chiyonofuji in second place on the all-time list.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2017/05/01/sumo/basho-reports/kisenosato-heads-summer-basho-top-ranked-fighter/#.WQsrkE2GPoo|title=Kisenosato heads into Summer Basho as top-ranked fighter|date=1 May 2017|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=4 May 2017}}</ref> He won the summer tournament or Natsu Basho, with a ''[[Glossary of sumo terms#zensho|zenshō]],'' a perfect 15–0 score.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201705270045.html|title=Hakuho wins for 1st time in a year and grabs his 38th career title|work=[[The Asahi Shimbun]]|access-date=2017-05-28|language=en-us}}</ref> Hakuhō entered the July tournament in Nagoya with two long-standing records in view: he needed nine wins to equal Chiyonofuji's career mark of 1045 and eleven to match Kaiō's all-time record of 1047. His task was made somewhat less challenging when Kakuryū, Kisenosato and Terunofuji withdrew with injuries in the first week. He drew level with Chiyonofuji with a win over [[Kagayaki Taishi|Kagayaki]] on day nine and moved ahead of the late yokozuna by beating [[Chiyoshoma]] (a pupil of Chiyonofuji) on the following day. After an upset loss to [[Mitakeumi]] on day 11 he recovered to beat Tamawashi and Takayasu to break Kaiō's all-time record.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2017/07/21/sumo/hakuhos-place-pantheon-greats-continues-grow/|title=Hakuho's place in pantheon of greats continues to grow|first=John|last=Gunning|date=21 July 2017|newspaper=The Japan Times}}</ref> He entered the final day one win ahead of the ''maegashira'' 8 [[Aoiyama]] and clinched his 39th title with a win over Harumafuji. After receiving his trophy he said "I lost on the 11th day, but the way I turned back the tide after that was in my head so I was able to get on the ring relaxed today. To be able to break Kaio's 1,047 record and Chiyonofuji's 1,045 mark at the same tournament is gratifying. I'd like to go back to my hometown and rest up tomorrow but the thing in my head right now is 1,000 wins in makuuchi."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://japantoday.com/category/sports/sumo-hakuho-wins-39th-career-title-at-nagoya-tournament|title=Hakuho wins 39th career title at Nagoya tournament|date=23 July 2017|newspaper=Japan Today}}</ref>

2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191031185301/http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201805270037.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

He withdrew on Day 4 of the July tournament at the beginning of the day's bouts, due to a right knee injury.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2018/07/11/sumo/basho-reports/kakuryu-suffers-first-defeat-injured-hakuho-pulls-nagoya-basho/#.W0YmEuSWymQ|title=Kakuryu suffers first defeat; injured Hakuho pulls out of Nagoya Basho|date=11 July 2018|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=11 July 2018}}</ref>

On 22 September 2018, the 14th day of the Aki Basho, Hakuhō created history by winning his 41st Emperor's Cup and scoring his 1000th victory in sumo's top ''makuuchi'' division.<ref name="Asahi Shimbun">{{cite news|url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201809220051.html|title=SUMO/ Hakuho clinches Autumn Grand Sumo tourney in 41st career title|date=22 September 2018|newspaper=[[The Asahi Shimbun]]|access-date=22 September 2018|archive-date=16 January 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200116045608/http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201809220051.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> The following day he completed the tournament undefeated, the 14th time he had achieved a ''zensho-yusho'', extending his own record.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20180923/p2g/00m/0sp/038000c|title=Sumo: Grand champion Hakuho wins Autumn meet with perfect record|date=23 September 2018|newspaper=The Mainichi|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180924142410/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20180923/p2g/00m/0sp/038000c|archive-date=24 September 2018}}</ref> His victory also meant he had won at least one top division championship for 13 consecutive years, beating the previous record of 12 held by Taihō.<ref name="heart">{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2018/10/31/sumo/sumo-dominance-heart-matter/#.W9sMV-SWyUk|author=Gunning, John|title=Sumo dominance down to heart over matter|date=31 October 2018|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=1 November 2018}}</ref>

He withdrew from the regional tour following the September tournament because of a recurrence of his right knee injury and underwent surgery on 18 October.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20181019/p2g/00m/0sp/022000c|title=Sumo: Grand champion Hakuho undergoes successful surgery on right knee|date=19 October 2018|newspaper=The Mainichi|access-date=19 October 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181020011535/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20181019/p2g/00m/0sp/022000c|archive-date=20 October 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref> He confirmed on 8 November that he was withdrawing from the November tournament because of persistent pain in the knee. This is the first year since 2006 that he has won only one tournament in a calendar year.<ref>{{cite news |title=Persistent knee injury forces Hakuho out of Kyushu tourney |url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201811080060.html |newspaper=Asahi Shimbun |access-date=8 November 2018 |date=8 November 2018 |archive-date=8 November 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181108143520/http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201811080060.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===2019===
Hakuhō won his first ten matches in January 2019, but then lost three in a row to [[Mitakeumi]], [[Tamawashi]] and [[Takakeishō]]. He withdrew from the tournament on Day 14, with his stablemaster revealing that Hakuhō injured his right knee on Day 4 and his left ankle on Day 5.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190126/p2g/00m/0sp/048000c|title=Sumo: Hakuho withdraws from New Year meet, ends bid for 42nd title|date=26 January 2019|publisher=The Mainichi|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190128185433/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190126/p2g/00m/0sp/048000c|archive-date=28 January 2019}}</ref> It was his 11th career withdrawal, and his fifth in the last seven tournaments.

At the March 2019 tournament, which was the last in the [[Heisei era]], Hakuhō won his 42nd top division ''yūshō'' with a perfect 15–0 record, his 15th ''zenshō-yūshō'' of his career. He sealed his perfect record with a hard-fought ''shitatenage'' victory over [[Kakuryū]] on the final day. Interviewed after his triumph he said "I entered sumo at the tournament in Osaka, and now I’m here as we ring out the end of the Heisei era, so Osaka has a special place in my heart. I am a creature of this era. Nine years ago in Nagoya, I received a letter from the emperor. This Heisei era means everything to me." Despite appearing to be in pain from an arm injury he then led the crowd in a [[tejime]], a clapping ceremony to mark the end of the era.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2019/03/24/sumo/basho-reports/perfect-hakuho-captures-42nd-title/#.XJe4KHdFyM8|title=Perfect Hakuho captures 42nd title|newspaper=The Japan Times|date=24 March 2019|access-date=24 March 2019}}</ref> This was seen as a breach of etiquette as the tournament ceremonies had not yet concluded, and after criticism by the [[Yokozuna Deliberation Council]] Hakuhō and his stablemaster apologized to the Sumo Association.<ref name="carpet">{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2019/03/68883b56bc4e-sumo-hakuho-called-on-carpet-for-unapproved-cheer-leading.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho called on carpet for unapproved cheer leading|date=28 March 2019|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=28 March 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190328171720/https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2019/03/68883b56bc4e-sumo-hakuho-called-on-carpet-for-unapproved-cheer-leading.html|archive-date=28 March 2019}}</ref> Hakuho was issued an official reprimand by the Sumo Association the following month for his actions.<ref name=NHK240419>{{cite web |url=https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20190424_33/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190424115451/https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20190424_33/|url-status=dead|archive-date=24 April 2019|title=Hakuho reprimanded for hand-clapping cheer|author=<!--Not stated--> |date=24 April 2019|website=www3.nhk.or.jp|publisher=NHK World-Japan|access-date=24 April 2019}}</ref>

Hakuhō revealed on 31 March that the injury suffered in the March tournament was a muscle tear in his upper right arm, and that he had opted for rest rather than surgery.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2019/03/31/sumo/hakuho-reveals-muscle-tear-upper-right-arm/#.XKIiEuSWyUk|title=Hakuho reveals muscle tear in upper right arm|date=31 March 2019|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=1 April 2019}}</ref> He only performed the ''yokozuna'' ''[[dohyo-iri]]'' ceremony during the spring tour, and on 9 May his stablemaster confirmed that he would be sitting out the Natsu tournament as he had only begun basic training routines.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190509/p2g/00m/0sp/054000c|title=Sumo: Arm injury forces yokozuna Hakuho to pull out of summer meet|date=9 May 2019|newspaper=The Mainichi|access-date=9 May 2019|archive-date=9 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190509143106/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190509/p2g/00m/0sp/054000c|url-status=dead}}</ref> He resumed full training at the beginning of June, and shortly before the Nagoya tournament declared himself fit to enter.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190705/p2g/00m/0sp/083000c|title=Sumo: All eyes on Hakuho as he returns from injury in Nagoya|date=5 July 2019|publisher=The Mainichi|access-date=5 July 2019|archive-date=5 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190705142916/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20190705/p2g/00m/0sp/083000c|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In the July tournament Hakuhō was defeated by [[Ichinojō]] and [[Kotoshōgiku]] but still had a chance to force a playoff on the final day against Kakuryū, who led him 13–1 to 12–2 on the leaderboard. However Kakuryū won the match, his third victory over Hakuhō in their last five meetings, and Hakuhō finished as runner-up alongside ''maegashira'' [[Terutsuyoshi]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2019/07/24/sumo/kakuryu-adds-solid-resume-latest-win/#.XUGK9-SWymQ|title=Kakuryu adds to solid resume with latest win|last=Gunning|first=John|date=24 July 2019|work=The Japan Times|access-date=31 July 2019}}</ref> It was his first runner-up performance since January 2016. He withdrew from the September 2019 tournament on the second day with a fractured finger.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201909090039.html|title=SUMO/ Hakuho drops out of Tokyo tourney with finger injury|date=9 September 2019|publisher=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=9 September 2019|archive-date=10 September 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190910034333/http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/AJ201909090039.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> He returned in November 2019 and won the tournament with a 14–1 record, three wins ahead of the rest of the field.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20191124/p2g/00m/0sp/039000c|title=Sumo: Hakuho puts final winning touch on 43rd championship|date=24 November 2019|work=The Mainichi|access-date=26 November 2019|archive-date=27 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191127222702/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20191124/p2g/00m/0sp/039000c|url-status=dead}}</ref> He told reporters after the tournament that he had feared he might never win another title, as his doctor had warned his March arm injury might never completely heal.<ref name="NHK1125">{{cite web|url=https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20191125_25/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191219145259/https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20191125_25/|url-status=dead|archive-date=19 December 2019|title=Hakuho aiming for 50 sumo tournament titles|date=25 November 2019|work=NHK World|access-date=26 November 2019}}</ref> He also said he was aiming for 50 championships before retiring.<ref name="NHK1125"/>

In December Hakuhō was nominated by [[Sumida, Tokyo|Sumida]], one of Tokyo's [[Special wards of Tokyo|wards]], to be a [[2020 Summer Olympics torch relay|torchbearer for the 2020 Olympics]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2019/12/d5c8cde52154-olympics-rugby-captain-leitch-hakuho-nominated-for-torch-relay.html|title=Olympics: Rugby captain Michael Leitch, Hakuho nominated for torch relay|date=8 December 2019|work=Kyodo News|access-date=10 December 2019}}</ref>

===2020===
Hakuhō withdrew from Day 4 of the January 2020 tournament, with his stablemaster citing a fever and inflammation of his lower back. He had suffered consecutive defeats to ''maegashira'' [[Endō Shōta|Endō]] and [[Myōgiryū Yasunari|Myōgiryū]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2020/01/15/sumo/hakuho-pulls-new-year-meet-losing-two-straight-bouts/#.Xh9JROSWyUk|title=Hakuho pulls out of New Year meet with fever after losing two straight bouts|date=15 January 2020|work=The Japan Times|access-date=15 January 2020}}</ref> Having pulled out of four tournaments in 2018 and three in 2019, he had not completed two tournaments in a row since May and July 2017.

At the March 2020 tournament, Hakuhō won his 44th top division ''yūshō'' with a 13–2 record.<ref name="asahi0322">{{cite web|url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/13235566|title=SUMO/ Hakuho's 44th title caps 1st 'empty' tourney after war|date=22 March 2020|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=23 March 2020}}</ref> On the last day he faced fellow ''yokozuna'' [[Kakuryū]] with both having a 12–2 score, the first time in seven years that two ''yokozuna'' had faced each other in the final match of the tournament with an identical record.<ref name="asahi0322"/> His victory took place in a virtually empty arena due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], the first time since 1945 that a tournament had been held without paying spectators.<ref name="asahi0322"/>

Hakuho withdrew from the July tournament due to an injury sustained to his right knee late in the tournament; he subsequently had [[endoscopy|endoscopic surgery]] on the knee and missed both the September and November tournaments as a result, the first time since his debut that he had missed two consecutive tournaments.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2020/09/9d636fdb34dd-sumo-grand-champions-hakuho-kakuryu-out-of-sept-tourney.html|title=Grand champions Hakuho, Kakuryu out of Sept. tourney|date=11 September 2020|publisher=Kyodo News}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2020/11/932afea43528-sumo-yokozuna-hakuho-withdraws-from-november-basho.html|publisher=Kyodo News|title=Yokozuna Hakuho withdraws from Nov. basho with knee injury|date=6 November 2020}}</ref>

Following the November 2020 ''basho'', Hakuhō - along with fellow ''yokozuna'' Kakuryū - were issued warnings by the Sumo Association's Yokozuna Deliberation Council due to lack of participation in recent sumo tournaments.<ref name=KYODO241120>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20201124/p2g/00m/0sp/026000c|title=Sumo: Advisory board issues warning to rehabbing yokozuna duo|date=24 November 2020|website=mainichi.jp|publisher=Kyodo|access-date=24 November 2020|archive-date=25 November 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201125045949/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20201124/p2g/00m/0sp/026000c|url-status=dead}}</ref> This is the middle of three notices that the council can issue between a letter of encouragement and a recommendation for retirement.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20181126/p2a/00m/0sp/018000c|title=Sumo yokozuna council issues rare 'encouragement' notice to struggling Kisenosato|date=26 November 2018|newspaper=Mainichi Daily News|publisher=Mainichi|access-date=24 November 2020|quote=In the event that a yokozuna's subpar performance continues for a period of time, the Yokozuna Deliberation Council, with over two-thirds agreement, issues one of three notices -- a request for the yokozuna to retire, a warning and one of encouragement, in order of severity.}}</ref> It is the first time in history that warning notices have been issued.<ref name=KYODO241120/>

===2021===
The Sumo Association announced on 5 January 2021 that Hakuhō tested positive for [[Coronavirus disease 2019|COVID-19]]. He had reported a [[Symptoms of COVID-19#Loss of smell|loss of the sense of smell]] the day before. The news broke with just five days until the scheduled start of the January 2021 Tournament.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Sumo: Grand champion Hakuho tests positive for coronavirus|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/01/b5f94746ca2e-breaking-news-sumo-grand-champion-hakuho-tests-positive-for-coronavirus.html|publisher=Kyodo News|date=5 January 2021|access-date=5 January 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/01/05/sports/hakuho-sumo-coronavirus.html|title=Sumo Champion Tests Positive, Worrying His Stable and His Sport|last=Mather|first=Victor|date=5 January 2021|website=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=5 January 2021}}</ref> Therefore he was absent from the tournament. Hakuhō left hospital on 14 January to continue his recovery at home.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://japantoday.com/category/sports/sumo-top-japan-sumo-wrestler-hakuho-released-from-hospital-after-covid-infection-media-reports|title=Yokozuna Hakuho released from hospital after COVID infection|date=15 January 2021|work=Japan Today|accessdate=9 February 2021|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20210209142317/https://japantoday.com/category/sports/sumo-top-japan-sumo-wrestler-hakuho-released-from-hospital-after-covid-infection-media-reports|archivedate=9 February 2021}}</ref> The whole of the [[Miyagino stable]] also sat out the tournament.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14083226|title=SUMO/ Top sumo wrestler Hakuho infected with coronavirus--JSA|date=5 January 2021|work=Asahi Shimbun|accessdate=22 March 2021}}</ref>

Upon the release of the March 2021 ''banzuke'' Hakuhō became the first wrestler to spend 100 straight tournaments in ''makuuchi'' from his top division debut (without ever falling to ''jūryō'').<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210301/p2g/00m/0sp/029000c|title=Sumo: Daieisho back among elite as Hakuho set for century milestone|date=1 March 2021|work=The Mainichi|accessdate=2 March 2021|archive-date=1 March 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210301040050/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210301/p2g/00m/0sp/029000c|url-status=dead}}</ref>

After winning his first two bouts of the March tournament, Hakuhō withdrew after doctors informed him that he needed surgery on his right [[patella|kneecap]] requiring approximately two months of rehabilitation, meaning that Hakuhō would miss the May 2021 ''basho''. Miyagino Oyakata said that Hakuhō would decide whether or not to continue competing based on his results in the July tournament.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/03/920c8e538150-sumo-hakuho-withdraws-from-tournament-over-knee-concerns.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho pulls out of meet, set to miss 2 months after surgery|date=16 March 2021|work=Kyodo News|accessdate=16 March 2021}}</ref> He underwent endoscopic surgery on 19 March. This was the same knee that had also been operated on in August of the previous year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210321/p2g/00m/0sp/069000c|title=Sumo: Yokozuna Hakuho's knee surgery performed on Friday|date=21 March 2021|work=The Mainichi|accessdate=22 March 2021|archive-date=21 March 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210321123817/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210321/p2g/00m/0sp/069000c|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 29 March, the Yokozuna Deliberation Council upheld the warning that was first issued to Hakuhō following the November 2020 tournament. The council announced it would revisit the issue at the end of the July 2021 tournament, which Hakuho referred to as "make or break" for his continued participation in active competition.<ref name=kyodo290321>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/03/77a9bf004ea0-sumo-hakuho-remains-subject-to-warning-over-injury-absences.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho remains subject to warning over injury absences|date=29 March 2021|work=Kyodo News|accessdate=29 March 2021}}</ref>

Following the retirement of Kakuryū, Hakuho was the sole ''yokozuna'' listed on the May 2021 ''banzuke.'' This marked the first time since September 2012 that there was only one active ''yokozuna'', and uniquely, Hakuhō was also the sole ''yokozuna'' on that occasion.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sumo.or.jp/EnHonbashoTopics/banzuke_topics/|title=2021 May Grand Sumo Tournament Banzuke Topics|work=Japan Sumo Association|access-date=30 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210430192945/http://sumo.or.jp/EnHonbashoTopics/banzuke_topics/|archive-date=30 April 2021}}</ref> He formally withdrew from the May tournament on 7 May, due to his knee surgery, making it the sixth straight tournament he would fail to complete.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210507/p2g/00m/0sp/062000c|title=Sumo: Yokozuna Hakuho to miss 6th straight basho|pages=The Mainichi|access-date=7 May 2021|archive-date=7 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210507041146/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20210507/p2g/00m/0sp/062000c|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Mounting a comeback in July 2021, Hakuhō went undefeated for the first 14 days of the competition and faced ''ōzeki'' [[Terunofuji]], who was also undefeated and vying for ''yokozuna'' promotion, in the final match of the tournament. Hakuhō defeated Terunofuji with an [[armlock]] throw (''kotenage'') to win his 45th top division championship and secure his 16th ''[[Glossary of sumo terms#zenshō|zenshō]]''. Hakuhō expressed relief after the match, saying that he did not expect to win the ''basho'' with a perfect record at his age.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/07/16c6b8e195c5-sumo-hakuho-defeats-terunofuji-wins-title-with-perfect-15-0-record.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho defeats Terunofuji, wins title with perfect 15-0 record|date=18 July 2021|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=18 July 2021}}</ref> His ''yūshō'' win at the age of 36 years and four months surpassed that of fellow ''yokozuna'' [[Chiyonofuji]], who won his final championship at 35 years and five months.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/backstories/1718/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210726050133/https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/backstories/1718/|url-status=dead|archive-date=26 July 2021|title=A tale of two sumo comebacks|last=Hiroshi|first=Morita|date=25 July 2021|work=NHK World - Japan|access-date=28 July 2021}}</ref> Speaking to reporters the next day, Hakuhō said, "The prospect for retirement was looming large, right before me, so I fired myself up more than before."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14398659|title=SUMO/ Relieved Hakuho says his career was on the line in Nagoya tourney|date=19 July 2021|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=16 August 2021}}</ref>

Hakuhō did not take part in practice sessions for the September 2021 tournament and gave no indication that he was ready to compete.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14448782|title=SUMO/ Sources: Hakuho to retire 1 win shy of 900 as a yokozuna|date=27 September 2021|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=27 September 2021}}</ref> In the event he was withdrawn from the tournament along with the rest of Miyagino stable after two wrestlers tested positive for [[COVID-19]]. It was the second time in 2021 that the stable had to withdraw from a ''basho'' because of a coronavirus outbreak.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/09/7ac627ff94b9-sumo-hakuho-to-miss-autumn-meet-with-stable-barred-over-covid-cases.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho to miss Autumn meet with stable barred over COVID cases|date=6 September 2021|publisher=Kyodo|access-date=6 September 2021}}</ref>

==Retirement==
Reports emerged after the September 2021 tournament that Hakuhō intended to retire, with his long-standing knee injuries leading him to believe he could no longer complete a 15 day tournament.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20210927_07/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210926220216/https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20210927_07/|url-status=dead|archive-date=26 which exceptional ''yokozuna'' are given special one-generation elder status and allowed them to keep their ''[[shikona]]'' after retirement, did not in fact exist.<ref>{{cite The Sumo Association's Board of Directors officially finalized Hakuhō's retirement on 30 September.<ref name=INTAI300921/>  Unusually, before approving Hakuhō's acquisition of the Magaki stock the Sumo Association required him to sign a pledge that he would carry out the duties of an elder and that he would not act in ways that went against the "spirit of the way of sumo."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14451823|title=SUMO/ Hakuho can take stablemaster name if he agrees to toe the line|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=1 October 2021}}</ref> At a press conference held the following day, Hakuhō told reporters that he was filled with a sense of relief. He said that he had made the decision to retire after achieving double-digit wins at the July tournament in [[Nagoya]], and waited for the right time because of several factors, including [[Terunofuji Haruo|Terunofuji]]'s promotion to ''yokozuna'', the Tokyo [[2020 Summer Olympics|Olympic]] and [[2020 Summer Paralympics|Paralympic]] Games, and the [[COVID-19]] outbreak that prevented his [[Miyagino stable|stable]] from competing in the September ''basho''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2021/10/00a28048043d-record-setting-yokozuna-hakuho-expresses-relief-at-ending-sumo-career.html|title=Record-setting yokozuna Hakuho expresses relief at ending sumo career|publisher=Kyodo News|date=1 October 2021|access-date=1 October 2021}}</ref> He was assigned to the [[Japan Sumo Association#Departments|Guidance and Promotion Department]] of the Sumo Association, until the division of duties was decided again in February 2022.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://hochi.news/articles/20211004-OHT1T51168.html|title=元横綱・白鵬の間垣親方は指導普及部で職務をスタートへ|date=4 October 2021|work=Hochi|language=Japanese|access-date=4 October 2021}}</ref> When the ''banzuke'' for the November 2021 tournament in Fukuoaka was released, there were only 41 wrestlers in the top division instead of the usual 42. Hakuhō's retirement announcement on September 30 had come after the ''banzuke'' committee meeting to draw up the new rankings, but an extra wrestler from ''jūryō'' was not promoted and instead Hakuhō's name was effectively blanked out from the ''yokozuna'' spot he would have occupied.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202110310001172.html|title=【九州場所新番付】白鵬引退影響か、適任者不在か？幕内力士が定員割れ|work=Nikkan Sports|language=Japanese|access-date=1 November 2021}}</ref> Hakuhō was seen at this tournament in the Sumo Association's blue security uniform, as it is customary for new ''[[Glossary of sumo terms#oyakata|oyakata]]'' to start out as a tournament security guard.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20211115/p2a/00m/0sp/019000c|title=Ex-yokozuna Hakuho begins new role, provides security at Kyushu sumo tournament| newspaper=Mainichi Daily News |date=15 November 2021|publisher=The Mainichi|access-date=16 November 2021}}</ref>

The ceremony was also marked by the performance of a {{nihongo|''sanbasō''|三番叟}}, a ceremonial dance for good omens, by the leading actor of [[kabuki]]'s Naritaya house, [[Ichikawa Danjūrō XIII]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://hochi.news/articles/20230128-OHT1T51042.html?page=1|title=元白鵬・宮城野親方の引退相撲、市川團十郎がオープニングセレモニーにサプライズ登場|date=28 January 2023|publisher=Sports ==Career as stablemaster==
On 28 July 2022 the [[Japan Sumo Association]] announced that he had inherited the Miyagino [[Toshiyori|elder stock]] and would become the  head coach of the stable, as the then Miyagino-''oyakata'' (former [[Chikubayama Masakuni|Chikubayama]]) would reach the mandatory retirement age of 65 in August 2022.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20220728/p2g/00m/0sp/053000c|title=Sumo: Ex-grand champ Hakuho to lead Miyagino stable|date=28 July 2022|work=The Mainichi|access-date=18 August 2022|archive-date=7 August 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220807211036/https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20220728/p2g/00m/0sp/053000c|url-status=dead}}</ref> The two swapped elder names, with the former Miyagino becoming Magaki-''oyakata'' and staying on as a consultant. Hakuhō had already recruited several wrestlers himself while still an active wrestler, including former ''maegashira'' {{Interlanguage link|Daikihō Masahiro|ja|3=大喜鵬将大|lt=Daikihō}}, popular lightweight [[Enhō Akira|Enhō]] and [[Ishiura Shikanosuke|Ishiura]], and the {{height|m=2}} tall Mongolian-born [[Hokuseihō Osamu|Hokuseihō]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2020/08/26/sumo/hakuho-hokuseiho-mongolia/|title=Hakuho may be on path to becoming great stablemaster if latest recruit pans out|last=Gunning|first=John|date=26 August 2020|work=Japan Times|access-date=30 September 2020}}</ref> Miyagino continued to recruit high-potential wrestlers, particularly in amateur circles. In September 2022, former amateur-''yokozuna'' [[Kihō Tomotaka|Kihō]] became the first wrestler recruited by Hakuhō since taking over the Miyagino name.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202208220000831.html|title=学生横綱新弟子・川副圭太に宮城野親方期待「小さい白鵬を見ている感じ。相撲もうまい」|language=ja|date=22 August 2022|work=Nikkan Sports|accessdate=18 July 2023}}</ref> In December of the same year, Miyagino recruited one of the stars of amateur competitions, two-time high school titleholder and corporate champion [[Hakuōhō Tetsuya|Hakuōhō]]. Nicknamed 'Reiwa Monster', Hakuōhō achieved record-breaking promotions and achievements in the space of just 7 months since his official debut as a professional wrestler.<ref>{{Cite news|url= https://www3.nhk.or.jp/news/html/20221201/k10013910231000.html |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20221201113759/http://www3.nhk.or.jp/news/html/20221201/k10013910231000.html |url-status= dead |archive-date= 1 December 2022 |script-title=ja: 2年連続 高校横綱 鳥取城北 落合哲也選手 宮城野部屋に入門へ |website=NHK |date=1 December 2022 |access-date=22 January 2023 |language=ja }}</ref> With 
{{interlanguage link|Tenshōhō Shingo|ja|3=天照鵬真豪|lt=Tenshōhō}}'s promotion to ''jūryō'' after the [[2023 in sumo#July|July 2023]] tournament, Miyagino successfully raised three new ''sekitori'' after only a little over a year at the head of his [[heya (sumo)|stable]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202307260000423.html?mode=all |script-title=ja: 向中野改め天照鵬が新十両、出身地の伊勢神宮から命名　元横綱白鵬の師匠は「令和の猛牛」と期待 |website=Nikkan Sports |date=1 December 2022 |access-date=12 September 2023|language=ja}}</ref> At a career conference in September 2023, Miyagino made no secret of his hopes to train two ''yokozuna'' himself during his coaching career.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://hochi.news/articles/20230912-OHT1T51109.html?page=1 |script-title=ja: 元白鵬の宮城野親方「横綱を２人ぐらい育てたい」　２２年後の還暦土俵入りへの夢語る|website=Sports Hochi|date=12 September 2023 |access-date=12 September 2023|language=ja}}</ref>

===Hokuseihō's violence scandal and Miyagino stable's closure===
In February 2024, it was announced by the Sumo Association that [[Hokuseihō Osamu|Hokuseihō]], the protégé of Hakuhō, had assaulted several stablemates and that an investigation had been launched after one of them made a formal complaint to the association. Hakuhō was summoned to inform him that disciplinary proceedings had been launched against both of them, risking a salary deduction and a demotion in the ''[[toshiyori]]'' hierarchy (the elders in charge of running the association).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2024/02/21/kiji/20240221s00005000441000c.html|title=暴行発覚の北青鵬 激闘制した翌日…初場所"突然"休場 診断書提出も宮城野親方は"意味深なコメント|date=21 February 2024|publisher=Sports Nippon|access-date=21 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.chunichi.co.jp/article/857061?rct=sumo|title=元白鵬の宮城野親方に2階級降格処分案、暴力行為の北青鵬に引退勧告へ 相撲協会、23日に理事会|date=21 February 2024|publisher=Chunichi Sports|access-date=21 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref> On 23 February, the Sumo Association met and accepted their Compliance Committee's report. The board accepted the resignation of Hokuseihō, who had submitted his retirement papers the previous day.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202402230000290.html|title=幕内北青鵬は引退届を受理 後輩力士への暴力行為、日本相撲協会の理事会で確認|date=23 February 2024 |publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=23 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref> Hakuhō told reporters after the board meeting "I'm so sorry for causing concern to the sumo association, fans and those who support me."<ref name=kyodo230224>{{cite web |url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2024/02/89fd0e352fa7-sumo-former-yokozuna-hakuho-penalized-over-proteges-violence.html|title=Sumo: Former yokozuna Hakuho penalized over protege's violence|date=23 February 2024|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=23 February 2024}}</ref>

Disciplinary decisions also targeted Hakuhō, because the Sumo Association found that he had learned about an assault committed by Hokuseihō that took place during the July 2020 tournament, but did not confirm the incident with either party involved nor report it to the association.<ref name="didntconfirm">{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202402230000317.html|title=元白鵬は暴行を「確認せず」「報告せず」「注意せず」宮城野親方に日本相撲協会が厳罰|date=23 February 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=9 March 2024|language=ja}}</ref> Hakuhō was demoted from ''iin'' (committee member) to the lowest ranking of ''toshiyori'' (elder) and received a salary cut of 20 percent for three months.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202402230000289.html|title=大横綱に厳罰処分 元白鵬の宮城野親方が２階級降格と減俸処分 暴力行為に対する監督責任|date=23 February 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=23 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref><ref name=ns_ichimon>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202402230000331.html|title=親方には任せられず…宮城野部屋４月以降は所属の伊勢ケ浜一門が預かることを検討|date=23 February 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=23 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref> In the comments to the report submitted to the association, the auditors wrote that had he acted as a stablemaster, it could have possibly prevented the further acts of violence that later occurred over the span of a year.<ref name="didntconfirm"/> The Sumo Association commented after the affair that more serious disciplinary sanctions could be applied in the event of a new scandal; with spokesman Shibatayama ([[List of yokozuna#62|the 62nd ''yokozuna'']] [[Ōnokuni Yasushi|Ōnokuni]]) telling reporters that a suggestion had been made at the Compliance Committee meeting to remove Hakuhō from the association entirely.<ref name=kyodo230224/>

Following publication of the association's investigation, the association decided to apply additional disciplinary sanctions with Hakuhō effectively removed as stablemaster of [[Miyagino stable]] in all but name for an indefinite period; the Sumo Association commenting at the time that he "lacks a great deal of knowledge and awareness as a master."<ref name=ns_ichimon/> In practice, the board decision led to the temporary appointment of Tamagaki (the former [[Tomonohana Shinya|Tomonohana]]), a member of the Isegahama ''[[Glossary of sumo terms#ichimon|ichimon]]'', to supervise Miyagino stable during the [[2024 in sumo#March|March 2024 tournament]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202402260000598.html|title=宮城野部屋の師匠代行に同じ伊勢ケ浜一門の玉垣親方を任命へ 元横綱白鵬の親方が師匠立場外れる|date=26 February 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=26 February 2024|language=ja}}</ref> After that tournament, the Sumo Association formally announced the closure of Miyagino stable and the transfer of all the wrestlers and coaches (including Hakuhō) to [[Isegahama stable (2007)|Isegahama stable]].<ref name=kyodo280324>{{cite web |url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2024/03/d826209db284-sumo-wrestlers-from-scandal-hit-miyagino-stable-to-join-isegahama.html|title=Sumo: Wrestlers from scandal-hit Miyagino stable to join Isegahama|date=28 March 2024|publisher=Kyodo News |access-date=28 March 2024}}</ref>

After the scandal, Hakuhō visited the families of all of his wrestlers to apologize to them and to alleviate their concerns,<ref name=pilgrimage>{{cite web |url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2024/05/05/kiji/20240505s00005000056000c.html|title=宮城野親方 "謝罪行脚"終え、指導にまい進|date=5 May 2024|publisher=Sports Nippon|access-date=5 May 2024|language=ja}}</ref> something he had been advised to do after a meeting with [[Asahifuji Seiya|Isegahama]] on 3 April.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202404030000877.html|title=宮城野親方が謝罪＆説明で全国行脚へ 弟子の「親御さんに会いにいきなさい」伊勢ケ浜親方が助言|date=3 April 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=3 April 2024|language=ja}}</ref> He reported that the families had shown signs of encouragement towards him.<ref name=pilgrimage/> During the May tournament, his actions within Isegahama stable received positive feedback, particularly with regard to his commitment during the training sessions, in which he took part fighting against the stable's ''sekitori''. In particular, his training against [[Takarafuji]] received positive comments, the latter having been one of the leading wrestlers during the first half of the tournament.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/column/sumo/news/202405190000370.html|title=伊勢ケ浜部屋に"最強コーチ"就任 部屋付き宮城野親方が身ぶり手ぶり交えて指導|date=20 May 2024|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=21 May 2024|language=ja}}</ref>

In early 2025, with the organization of his eponymous amateur tournament (the [[Hakuhō Cup]]) confirmed, it was interpreted by the press that staging the event could be a first step towards Miyagino's rehabilitation, since holding the event at Ryōgoku Kokugikan required the green light from the Japan Sumo Association and Miyagino's guardian, stablemaster Isegahama (the former [[Asahifuji Seiya|Asahifuji]]).<ref name=gunning2025>{{cite web|last=Gunning|first=John|author-link=John Gunning (journalist)|title=Amateur sumo world caught by surprise as Hakuho Cup set to go ahead|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2024/12/18/sumo/hakuho-cup-to-go-ahead/|work=[[The Japan Times]]|url-access=subscription|date=18 December 2024|access-date=9 January 2025|archive-date=9 January 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250109070851/https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2024/12/18/sumo/hakuho-cup-to-go-ahead/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Resignation===
In April 2025 both Miyagino and Isegahama denied reports from multiple Japanese tabloids, including ''[[Shūkan Bunshun]]'' and ''[[Shūkan Shinchō]]'', that Miyagino was planning to fully retire from sumo after the May 2025 tournament.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mainichi.jp/articles/20250410/k00/00m/050/046000c|title=大相撲の宮城野親方 週刊文春「日本相撲協会退職」報道を否定|date=10 April 2025|publisher=Mainichi Shimbun|access-date=10 April 2025|language=ja}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202504100000507.html|title=元白鵬の宮城野親方が退職意向の報道をあらためて否定「退職は完全にない？」の問いに「はい」|date=10 April 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=10 April 2025|language=ja}}</ref> 

Although Miyagino again denied the reports when asked, upon the conclusion of that tournament,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202505250002576.html|title=宮城野親方「はい、ないです」週刊誌の退職報道をあらためて否定|date=25 May 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=25 May 2025|language=ja}}</ref> the daily sports newspaper ''[[Sports Nippon]]'' published an article on May 29 stating that Miyagino had indeed submitted a resignation request, but that it had not been immediately taken into account by the board of directors.<ref name=nipponresign>{{cite web|url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2025/05/30/kiji/20250530s00005000002000c.html|title=宮城野親方 退職届を提出 6月2日に臨時理事会で話し合いも本人の意思固く|date=29 May 2025|publisher=Sports Nippon|access-date=29 May 2025|language=ja}}</ref> His decision would date back to the March 2025 tournament, where he would have already announced his decision to make it official in the spring.<ref name=nikkanresign>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202505300001545.html?Page=1|title=元横綱白鵬の宮城野親方、相撲協会退職濃厚 伊勢ケ浜親方に退職届渡し提出委ねる 2日にも受理|date=30 May 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=30 May 2025|language=ja}}</ref> According to reports, Miyagino had numerous exchanges with Asakayama (the former [[Kaiō Hiroyuki|Kaiō]]), board director for the Isegahama clan, and with Isegahama himself, who sought to dissuade him from resigning.<ref name=nikkanresignjune>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202506020000724.html|title=宮城野親方退職、協会発表「９日付の願を受理」伊勢ケ浜親方が再開示唆して慰留も本人の意志固く|language=ja|date=2 June 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=2 June 2025}}</ref>

On the same day as the ''Sports Nippon'' article, the Sumo Association declared at an ordinary meeting of its board that the question of allowing Miyagino to be released from his punishment would be discussed at an extraordinary meeting, the date of which was set on 2 June to coincide with the definitive retirement of Isegahama (the former {{Transliteration|ja|yokozuna}} Asahifuji).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202505290000882.html|title=退職報道が相次ぐ元白鵬に関する話し合いナシ 臨時理事会で伊勢ケ浜部屋継承を含めて話し合いへ|date=29 May 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=29 May 2025|language=ja}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2025/05/29/kiji/20250529s00005000293000c.html|title=日本相撲協会 宮城野部屋の処遇について6月2日臨時理事会開催へ 昨年4月の当面閉鎖から1年強|date=29 May 2025|publisher=Sports Nippon|access-date=29 May 2025|language=ja}}</ref> Still according to ''Sports Nippon'', Miyagino had already made arrangements with his backers and sponsors, however, refusing to admit his resignation publicly to avoid overshadowing [[Ōnosato Daiki|Ōnosato]]'s promotion to {{Transliteration|ja|yokozuna}} which took place during the same period.<ref name=nipponresign/>

Among the reasons cited by ''Sports Nippon''{{'}}s sources to explain Miyagino's resignation, was the likelihood that he would remain under the tutelage of Isegahama stable after the retirement of stablemaster Isegahama (the former {{Transliteration|ja|yokozuna}} [[Asahifuji Seiya|Asahifuji]]) and under the authority of his successor, [[Terunofuji Haruo|Terunofuji]], who is Miyagino's junior in terms of both achievements and coaching career and with whom he does not have a good relationship.<ref name=nipponresign/><ref>{{cite web|last1=Takahara|first1=Kanako|last2=Kaneko|first2=Karin|title=Former yokozuna Hakuho to leave sumo association|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2025/06/02/sumo/hakuho-to-leave-jsa/|work=[[The Japan Times]]|url-access=subscription|date=2 June 2025|access-date=3 January 2025}}</ref> Other sources also reported on the uncertain status of the masters of the former Miyagino stable, who were apparently left in doubt as to the conditions for reopening the stable, with Asakayama declaring that the clan had been trying since early 2025 to reopen the stable, without success.<ref name=nikkanresign/><ref name=nikkanresignjune/> Coaches from Miyagino stable were also subject to a ten-year ban on holding positions on the association's board of directors and on recruiting wrestlers while the stable remained closed.<ref name=nikkanresign/>

On 2 June 2025 the Sumo Association unanimously decided that Miyagino's resignation would be considered as accepted on 9 June. It was reported that Miyagino would address the press on that day.<ref name=nikkanresignjune/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.chunichi.co.jp/article/1076169|title=元横綱白鵬・宮城野親方が日本相撲協会退職、9日に記者会見を予定【大相撲】|language=ja|date=2 June 2025|publisher=Chunichi Shimbun|access-date=2 June 2025}}</ref> Following the announcement, details were given on the fate of the wrestlers who were part of his former stable and on his [[toshiyori|elder stock]]. The Miyagino name should in fact be inherited by Isegahama (the former Asahifuji) in a context of re-employment after the mandatory age of 65 and in a context that should see him cede his stable and his Isegahama name to his pupil Terunofuji. According to the press conference, this choice is explained by Miyagino's desire to see his former apprentices supervised, as they should now belong permanently to the Isegahama stable.<ref name=nikkanresignjune/>

On 9 June 2025, the date of his departure from the Sumo Association, Hakuhō held a press conference in which he expressed his desire to remain involved with the sport from the outside. "I want to develop sumo from an outside perspective," he said. "I want to share sumo's appeal with many people around the world, not just in Japan." He told reporters that he had struggled in his decision to quit sumo's governing body, considering the impact it would have on the wrestlers he had been training. He also told reporters when asked that he would have had "no problem at all" if he had worked under new Isegahama stablemaster [[Terunofuji Haruo|Terunofuji]].<ref name=kyodo_departure>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2025/06/e2536ed7e2b3-sumo-great-hakuho-plans-to-promote-sport-from-outside-ruling-body.html|title=Sumo great Hakuho plans to promote sport from outside ruling body|date=9 June 2025|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=9 June 2025}}</ref>

==After professional sumo==
It was reported after Hakuhō's departure from the Japan Sumo Association in June 2025 that Hakuhō owned a plot of land in Tokyo's [[Chūō, Tokyo|Chuo ward]], initially meant to be the home of a new Miyagino stable, that he would likely use for a building related to a new sumo-related company. The building would include a sumo ring on the ground floor that could be seen from the outside through glass walls.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202506100001297.html|title=白鵬翔さん、新会社のビルを日本橋に建設へ ガラス張りの土俵作成案も、幻の新部屋を建設予定地|language=ja|date=11 June 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=11 June 2025}}</ref> On 14 June Hakuhō announced the establishment of his new sumo promotion company, Hakuhō Dayan Sumo & Sports, named in part for Mongolian emperor [[Dayan Khan]] who reigned from 1480 until 1517. The company expects to receive support from long-time Hakuhō supporter and [[Toyota]] chairman [[Akio Toyoda]], who was named to lead the amateur Japan Sumo Federation on the same day.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2025/06/4fcf3127e407-update2-sumo-hakuho-reveals-company-toyota-chairman-heads-amateurs-body.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho reveals company, Toyota chairman heads amateurs' body|date=14 June 2025|publisher=Kyodo News|access-date=14 June 2025}}</ref>

At the end of October 2025, it was announced that he would be one of the winners of the HEROs AWARD, alongside [[Megumi Ikeda]]. The prize is delivered by the [[Nippon Foundation]] and aimed at recognizing athletes who promote social action through sport for his work with children's sumo wrestling.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202510270001192.html|title=元横綱の白鵬翔さんが「HEROs AWARD 2025」を受賞「白鵬杯」など評価|date=27 October 2025|publisher=Nikkan Sports|lang=ja|access-date=28 October 2025}}</ref> During the ceremony, he praised the success of his [[Hakuhō Cup]] tournament, noting that both current {{translit|ja|yokozuna}} ([[Ōnosato Daiki|Ōnosato]] and [[Hōshōryū Tomokatsu|Hōshōryū]]) were former participants, and reiterated his goal of making sumo an [[Olympic sport]] by promoting women's participation in the sport.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2025/12/15/articles/20251215s00005000276000c.html|title=白鵬氏「男女平等でなければ…」「毎日ワクワクドキドキ」五輪種目へ決意 相撲グランドスラム構想にも言及|date=15 December 2025|publisher=Sports Nippon|lang=ja|access-date=16 December 2025}}</ref>

==Fighting style==
Hakuhō had a straightforward but effective style, reminiscent of ''yokozuna'' [[Takanohana Kōji|Takanohana]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/ss20060330a1.html|title=Mongolian Hakuho promoted to ozeki|date=30 March 2006|newspaper=[[The Japan Times]]|access-date=19 October 2006}}{{Dead link|date=July 2024 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> He has been called the "quintessential all-round sumo wrestler" because of his strength in both grappling and pushing techniques, and his ability to read and respond to his opponent's intentions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://newsonjapan.com/html/newsdesk/article/119250.php|title=Sumo: Hakuho holds the key as era of 4 yokozuna gets under way|date=9 March 2017|publisher=NewsOnJapan.com|access-date=13 March 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170312062636/http://newsonjapan.com/html/newsdesk/article/119250.php|archive-date=12 March 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> However, he said he did not have a special or favourite ''[[kimarite]]'', and that "the only thing I am very good at is ''yorikiri'' (force-out)".<ref name=student/> This technique, the most common ''kimarite'' in sumo, was used by Hakuhō to win 28% of his matches.<ref>{{cite web|title=Hakuho bouts by kimarite|publisher= Sumo Reference|url=http://sumodb.sumogames.de/Rikishi_kim.aspx?r=1123|access-date=30 April 2009}}</ref> He favoured the ''migi-yotsu'' position, meaning his right hand was inside and his left hand outside his opponent's arms, and he became famous for his left hand outside grip.<ref>{{cite web|publisher=Sumo Fan Magazine|access-date=3 August 2009|url=http://www.sumofanmag.com/content/Issue_13/Kimarite_Focus.pdf|title=Kimarite Focus|last=Mattila|first=Mikko|date=June 2007|archive-date=16 February 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120216071932/http://www.sumofanmag.com/content/Issue_13/Kimarite_Focus.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> He also regularly employed ''uwate-nage'', or overarm throw. While meeting foreign journalists in April 2009, Hakuhō said he was studying the techniques of the 35th ''yokozuna'' [[Futabayama Sadaji|Futabayama]], a wrestler whom he greatly admires, in particular his ''[[Go no sen|go-no-sen]]'' approach to the ''[[tachi-ai]]'' or initial charge.<ref name=student/><ref name="legacy">{{cite web|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2021/09/28/sumo/hakuho-legacy-retire-sumo/|title=Hakuho leaves unmatchable legacy after 20 years of dominance|date=28 September 2021|work=Japan Times|access-date=29 September 2021}}</ref> He also tried to use [[Wakanohana Kanji I|Wakanohana I]]'s trademark move of ''yobimodoshi'', or pulling body slam, in his own bouts.<ref name="legacy"/>

Hakuhō was criticized for giving his opponents an unnecessary final shove after the bout was already over (''dameoshi'').<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/sports/view/yokozuna-council-gives-hakuho-mixed-review-passes-on-kisenosato|title=Yokozuna council gives Hakuho mixed review; passes on Kisenosato|date=29 March 2016|newspaper=Japan Today|access-date=29 March 2016}}{{dead link|date=October 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.japantoday.com/smartphone/view/kuchikomi/hakuho-wins-33rd-title-but-its-lonely-at-the-top|title=Hakuho wins 33rd title, but it's lonely at the top|date=5 February 2015|newspaper=Japan Today|access-date=6 June 2016}}</ref> He did this on Day 4 of the March 2016 tournament in his win over [[Okinoumi]] and again on Day 8, when he sent [[Yoshikaze]] crashing into the ring side [[judge (sumo)|judge]] [[Sakahoko Nobushige|Izutzu Oyakata]], fracturing Izutzu's left [[femur]] and putting him out of action for three months.<ref name="warning">{{cite news|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/03/21/sumo/basho-reports/yokozuna-hakuho-apologizes-for-reckless-act-after-warning/#.VvFPvmf2aM8|title=Yokozuna Hakuho apologizes for reckless act after warning|date=21 March 2016|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=22 March 2016}}</ref> Hakuhō was warned by the head of the judging committee, [[Asahifuji Seiya|Isegahama Oyakata]], who said, "he has to understand completely that (such) redundant finishing blows are dangerous."<ref name="warning"/> Hakuhō apologized, calling his actions "inexcusable... Even I have weaknesses in the ring that I want to correct."<ref name="warning"/> Speaking after his tournament victory in May 2016 he commented, "I don’t know why but when I get into the ring, I turn into a different Hakuho. I think there are two Hakuhos. I'm gentler when I'm not in the ring."<ref name="perfect"/>

The [[Yokozuna Deliberation Council]] criticized him in January 2018 for using slaps to the face (''harite'') and leading with the forearm and elbow (''kachiage'') at the ''[[tachi-ai]]''; while both moves are technically legal, they are usually considered beneath the dignity of a ''yokozuna''.<ref name="asahi0118"/> Hakuhō continued to use these tactics, however, and was condemned once again by the Council after his championship-clinching win over Terunofuji in July 2021.<ref name="asahi0720">{{cite web|url=https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14399422|title=SUMO/ Yokozuna council butts heads with Hakuho over match tactics|date=20 July 2021|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=20 July 2021}}</ref> Hakuho responded to criticism of his style in this match and in the previous day's win over [[Shodai Naoya|Shōdai]], in which he stood far back at the ''tachi-ai'' and kept his distance throughout the match, by saying he was trying to protect his weak right knee.<ref name="asahi0720"/>

==Personal life==
In February 2007 Hakuhō married Sayoko Wada, then 22 years old, a Japanese university student and [[glamour model]],<ref>[http://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/special/2007sumouharu/KFullNormal20070323079.html "Sponichi Annex Sports Ohzumo 2007 Spring-basyo : Hakuho wins 11 times in a row"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110111084540/http://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/special/2007sumouharu/KFullNormal20070323079.html |date=11 January 2011 }} [[Sponichi|Sponichi Annex]] 23 March 2007 {{in lang|ja}}</ref> after a three-year relationship. The wedding ceremony took place at [[Meiji Shrine]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantoday.com/category/picture-of-the-day/view/hakuhos-wedding|title=Hakuho's wedding|date=22 February 2010|work=[[Japan Today]]|access-date=22 February 2010}} {{Dead link|date=January 2015}}</ref> The couple have four children.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.asahi.com/sp/ajw/articles/AJ201909030047.html|title=SUMO/ Hakuho gets Japanese citizenship, eyes managing stable|date=3 September 2019|work=Asahi Shimbun|access-date=22 April 2020}}{{Dead link|date=July 2024 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

In July 2017 he was reported to be considering taking [[Japanese citizenship]], which is required to stay in the [[Japan Sumo Association]] as an [[toshiyori|elder]] after retirement.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2017/07/5f00a9101c6b-focus-sumo-hakuho-eying-japanese-citizenship.html|title=Sumo: Hakuho eying Japanese citizenship|date=20 July 2017|publisher=Kyodo News|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170724142627/https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2017/07/5f00a9101c6b-focus-sumo-hakuho-eying-japanese-citizenship.html|archive-date=24 July 2017}}</ref> This would involve giving up Mongolian citizenship, which Hakuhō was reportedly reluctant to do while his father [[Jigjidiin Mönkhbat]] was still living.<ref name="jt050918">{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2018/05/09/sumo/future-without-sumo-unthinkable-hakuho/#.WvM-NeSWymQ|title=Future without sumo unthinkable for Hakuho|last=Gunning|first=John|date=9 May 2018|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=9 May 2018}}</ref> After Mönkhbat's death in April 2018 Hakuhō's mother denied that her husband had been opposed to the idea and said she felt Hakuhō's change in nationality was inevitable.<ref name="jt050918"/> It was reported in April 2019 that Hakuhō had begun the process by applying for renunciation of his Mongolian citizenship,<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2019/04/17/sumo/yokozuna-hakuho-takes-step-toward-japanese-citizenship/#.XLcmiOSWymQ|title=Yokozuna Hakuho takes step toward Japanese citizenship|date=17 April 2019|newspaper=The Japan Times|access-date=17 April 2019}}</ref> and he announced in September 2019 that he had acquired Japanese citizenship. He chose to change his legal name to his ''[[shikona]]'', Hakuhō Shō.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20190903_16/|title=Yokozuna Hakuho obtains Japanese citizenship|date=3 September 2019|access-date=3 September 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190903051859/https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20190903_16/|archive-date=3 September 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Other activities==
Since [[2010 in sumo|2010]] Hakuhō has hosted the [[Hakuhō Cup]], an annual amateur sumo event for elementary to middle school children, from both inside and outside Japan, and held at the [[Ryōgoku Kokugikan]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://tachiai.org/2018/02/14/rikishi-of-the-future-the-hakuho-cup-2018/|title=Rikishi of the Future – The Hakuho Cup 2018|date=14 February 2018|work=tachiai.org|access-date=3 February 2020}}</ref> In [[2024 in sumo|January 2024]] he also announced that he would be the sponsor of the first Dream Girls Cup, a sumo competition open to girls from first grade of elementary school to third grade of high school. The tournament, held in February, is also promoted by [[Shōko Kanazawa]] and aims to promote [[women's sumo]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://hochi.news/articles/20240122-OHT1T51102.html?page=1|script-title=ja:女子相撲大会「ドリームガールズ杯」２・１１に初開催　応援サポーターに就任した宮城野親方のまな娘や須藤弥勒も出場予定|work=Sports Hochi|date=22 January 2024|access-date=22 January 2024|language=ja}}</ref>
