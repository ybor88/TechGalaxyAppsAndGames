'''Gevorg "Giorgio" Petrosyan''' ({{langx|hy|Գևորգ Պետրոսյան}}; born December 10, 1985) is an Armenian-Italian former [[Kickboxing|kickboxer]] who competed in the [[middleweight]] division. After immigrating to Italy from Armenia at thirteen years old, Petrosyan began his professional career as a [[Muay Thai]] fighter at sixteen, starting out as a -54&nbsp;kg/119&nbsp;lb [[bantamweight]] before moving up through the weight classes and settling at the -70&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb middleweight division, taking numerous titles along the way. Following a decision loss to Nonthanan Por. Pramuk at [[Lumpinee Stadium]] in January 2007, he went on a six-year, forty-two fight undefeated streak considered to be one of the most dominant reigns in the history of the sport which was eventually ended by [[Andy Ristie]] at ''[[Glory 12: New York]]'' in November 2013.

His switch to kickboxing rules and subsequent ascent began in 2008 when he began competing for [[It's Showtime (kickboxing)|It's Showtime]] and [[K-1]], and he established himself as the world's top middleweight with two consecutive K-1 World MAX World Championship Tournament Championships in [[K-1 World MAX 2009 World Championship Tournament Final|2009]] and [[K-1 World MAX 2010 World Championship Tournament Final|2010]]. He then joined [[Glory (kickboxing)|Glory]] in 2012 following It's Showtime and K-1's demise and further cemented his place as the elite -70&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb kickboxer by winning the [[Glory 3: Rome - 2012 Lightweight Slam Final 8|Glory 2012 Lightweight Slam]] tournament.

==Early life==
Petrosyan was Born in [[Yerevan]], [[Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic]], [[Soviet Union]] (now [[Armenia]]) to Armenian parents on December 10, 1985, as Gevorg Petrosyan, he emigrated to [[Italy]] at the age of thirteen with his father, Andranik, and older brother, Stepan, arriving [[Illegal immigration|illegally]] in the country in the back of a truck. They initially lived [[Homelessness|homeless]] in [[Milan]], sleeping in train stations and on the streets, but were later taken in by a family in [[Gorizia]] who offered them work as night watchmen at their factory. After settling in Italy, he was soon joined by his mother, Karine, sister, Lianna, and younger brother, [[Armen Petrosyan (kickboxer)|Armen]], who is also a world champion kickboxer.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.avvenire.it/Sport/Pagine/petrosyan.aspx|title=Petrosyan, l'armeno azzurro|work=avvenire.it|date=9 October 2012|access-date=14 April 2013|archive-date=8 October 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131008230632/http://www.avvenire.it/Sport/Pagine/petrosyan.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Inspired by [[Bruce Lee]] and [[Jean-Claude Van Damme]] martial arts films, Petrosyan decided to begin training in [[Muay Thai]] at fourteen years old but was initially turned away from the gym for being too small.<ref name="Interview of Giorgio Petrosyan by Serge Trefeu (2009)">{{cite web|url=http://www.siamfightmag.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=372:giorgio-petrosyan-italy&catid=16&Itemid=164&lang=en|title=GIORGIO PETROSYAN (ITALY)|work=siamfightmag.com}}</ref> He persisted, however, and eventually started his training under Alfio Romanut at the Satori Gladiatorium Nemesis gym and had his first fight at the age of sixteen.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.giorgiopetrosyan.com/biografia/?lang=en|title=Biography|access-date=January 30, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110129080308/http://www.giorgiopetrosyan.com/biografia/?lang=en|archive-date=January 29, 2011}}</ref><ref name="Gevorg Petrosyan interview">{{cite web| url=http://www.kickbox.nl/forums/archive/topic/22983-1.html| title=Gevorg Petrosyan interview| access-date=2008-06-01| publisher=www.fansofk-1.com| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090505184811/http://www.kickbox.nl/forums/archive/topic/22983-1.html| archive-date=2009-05-05| url-status=dead}}</ref> He continues to reside in Gorizia in [[Northern Italy]] and worked as a [[bricklayer]] early in his career.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.jsports.co.jp/press/column/article/N2010100112421907.html |title=ĄÚĽŐĽĄĽ¤ĽżĄź¤ż¤Á¤ÎÁÇ´éĄŰ Âč2˛óĄĄĽ¸ĽçĽëĽ¸ĽŞĄŚĽÚĽČĽíĽˇĽ˘Ľó : ĽłĽéĽŕ |publisher=J Sports |access-date=2015-08-17 |archive-date=2011-07-19 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110719161349/http://www.jsports.co.jp/press/column/article/N2010100112421907.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Career==

===Muay Thai beginnings and early career (2002–2006)===
Giorgio Petrosyan started his career as a -54&nbsp;kg/119&nbsp;lb [[bantamweight]] at the age of sixteen and defeated a local Italian fighter in his February 2002 debut, breaking his toes while doing so.<ref name="Interview of Giorgio Petrosyan by Serge Trefeu (2009)"/> He turned professional shortly after and would compete almost exclusively under [[Muay Thai]] rules for the next five years. In his first title fight on November 9, 2003, he scored a third round [[knockout]] over Gionata Zarbo to be crowned the Muay Thai Association (MTA) Italian national champion. Steadily moving up in weight and competition level, he captured the MTA European -65&nbsp;kg/143&nbsp;lb Championship by [[outpoint]]ing [[Fabio Pinca]] in 2004 before making his only successful defence of the belt in February 2005 with another points win over [[Shemsi Beqiri]] in his home town of Gorizia. He fought to a [[Draw (tie)|draw]] with three-time [[Lumpinee Stadium]] champion Pinsinchai in [[Bologna]], Italy on April 10, 2005, but soon returned to the win column and defeated Olivier Tchétché by second round [[technical knockout]] in a [[World Muaythai Council|WMC]] Intercontinental Welterweight (-66.7&nbsp;kg/147&nbsp;lb) Championship match in [[Nova Gorica]], [[Slovenia]] on June 25, 2005.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.megathai.it/italia/menu%20portale/articoli_pubblicati/2005/luglio/petrosian/petrosian.htm |title=attreazzura da boxe scontatissima |publisher=Megathai.it |date=2005-06-30 |access-date=2015-08-17 |archive-date=2018-10-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181008114113/http://www.megathai.it/italia/menu%20portale/articoli_pubblicati/2005/luglio/petrosian/petrosian.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Petrosyan then made the journey to [[Thailand]] to train, visiting the WMC Samui Gym in [[Ko Samui (city)|Ko Samui]] and Tor. Silachai in [[Nakhon Ratchasima]]. He stopped Thai fighter Petch with a [[low kick]] in round three at Phetch Buncha Samui Stadium in Ko Samui in February 2006 before returning to Italy to compete in the ''Italian Extreme IV'' four-man tournament in [[Modena]] on April 1, 2006, which he ran through, finishing both opponents in the first round. He floored Richard Barnhill with a left [[Hook (boxing)|hook]] before putting him away with a low kick in the semi-finals and gave an almost identical performance in the final when he dispatched Roel Rink with a leg kick shortly after knocking him down with a right hook. This also marked the third consecutive fight in which Petrosyan had stopped opponents with low kicks. He became a [[World championship|world champion]] for the first time on June 10, 2006, by knocking out Benito Caupain with a first round high kick to win the vacant Kombat League World -66.7&nbsp;kg/147&nbsp;lb Muay Thai Championship in Nova Gorica. He would go on to successfully defend this title three times before the end of the year; TKOing Johnny Tancray in round four in [[Sardinia]], Italy on August 5, KOing Frankie Hudders in round two in [[Pordenone]], Italy on September 5 and winning a [[unanimous decision]] over Imro Main in [[Enschede]], [[Netherlands]] on November 19.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/238/gevorg-petrosyan-vince-il-titolo-mondiale-kl-contro-imro-main.htm|title=Petrosyan Vince il Titolo Mondiale kl Contro Imro|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226204158/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/238/gevorg-petrosyan-vince-il-titolo-mondiale-kl-contro-imro-main.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref>

Moving up to [[middleweight]], Petrosyan marked his arrival in the weight class that he would go on to dominate by winning the eight-man 72&nbsp;kg/158&nbsp;lb tournament at ''Janus Fight Night 2006'' in [[Padua]], Italy, on December 2, 2006. He took a unanimous decision win against Cédric Muller in the quarter-finals and TKO'd Frane Radnić in the semis, knocking him down twice in round one with a left [[Cross (boxing)|cross]] to the [[solar plexus]], before beating [[Marco Piqué]] via another unanimous decision in the final.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/253/petrosyan-continua-la-sua-marcia-inarrestabile.htm|title=Petrosyan Continua la Sua Marcia Inarrestabile|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226203656/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/253/petrosyan-continua-la-sua-marcia-inarrestabile.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/257/-janus-fight-night--2006-i-risultati-ufficiali.htm|title=Janus Fight Night 2006 Official Results|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226203803/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/257/-janus-fight-night--2006-i-risultati-ufficiali.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref>

===First defeat and subsequent kickboxing breakthrough (2007–2008)===
Petrosyan fought at the historic [[Lumpinee Stadium]] in [[Bangkok]], Thailand for the first time on January 23, 2007, where he was also handed the first loss of his career, dropping a decision to Nonthanan Por. Pramuk. In an interview, Petrosyan said the promoters required him to drink an extra two litres of water after he weighed in at 70&nbsp;kg, two kilograms below the agreed upon weight of 72&nbsp;kg. The drink given to Petrosyan caused him to have stomach cramps and made it very difficult for him to fight, though he decided to go through with it anyway. After the bout, Nonthanan retired, quickly ending the possibility of a rematch.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=Giorgio Petrosyan: Top of the World |url=https://themeparkatitsdarkest.com/2013/08/25/giorgio-petrosyan-top-of-the-world/ |magazine=International Kickboxer Magazine |date=July–August 2013 }}</ref>

He was able to bounce back from this loss by recording back-to-back wins, a third-round TKO of Sadio Cissoko on March 17, 2007, and a unanimous decision over Arslan Magomedov on April 14, 2007, at ''[[K-1 Italy Oktagon 2007]]'', both fights taking place in [[Milan]], Italy.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.megathai.it/italia/menu%20portale/articoli_pubblicati/2005/novembre/petro/palestra%20bologna%20kick%20boxing.htm |title=palestra bologna a bologna bolognesi |publisher=Megathai.it |date=2005-10-28 |access-date=2015-08-17 |archive-date=2016-03-04 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304054030/http://www.megathai.it/italia/menu%20portale/articoli_pubblicati/2005/novembre/petro/palestra%20bologna%20kick%20boxing.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref> From there, he was offered the biggest fight of his career at that point when he replaced [[John Wayne Parr]], who withdrew after injuring his ribs training with [[Nathan Corbett]], to challenge [[Buakaw Por. Pramuk]] for his WMC World Junior Middleweight (-69.8&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb) Championship at ''[[K-1 Fighting Network Scandinavian Qualification 2007]]'' in [[Stockholm]], [[Sweden]] on May 19, 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mymuaythai.com/archives/jwp-out-gevorg-petrosyan-in/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070422031852/http://www.mymuaythai.com/archives/jwp-out-gevorg-petrosyan-in/|url-status=usurped|archive-date=April 22, 2007|title=JWP Out Gevorg Petrosyan In!|work=mymuaythai.com}}</ref> After five rounds, the fight was ruled a [[split draw]] and, consequently, Buakaw retained his title.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/477/giorgio-petrosyan-vicino-al-colpaccio-in-svezia.htm|title=Giorgio Petrosyan Vicino al Colpaccio in Svezia|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226203542/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/477/giorgio-petrosyan-vicino-al-colpaccio-in-svezia.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref>

Petrosyan then went on to defeat [[Abdallah Mabel]] twice within two months in his next couple of fights, decisioning him in Padua on May 26, 2007, and then TKOing him in Trieste on July 27, 2007. After a first-round knockout of Behrouz Rastagar in [[Arnhem]], Netherlands on October 13, 2007, he entered the ''Janus Fight Night 2007'' -72&nbsp;kg/158&nbsp;lb tournament (the event he had won a year previously) on November 24, 2007. In the quarter-finals, he beat [[José Reis (kickboxer)|José Reis]] by unanimous decision. Then, in the semis, he met Abdallah Mabel for the third time in six months and bested the Frenchman once again, winning another unanimous decision. In the final, Petrosyan defeated another familiar opponent by unanimous decision, Marco Piqué, who he had also faced in the final the previous year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/756/janus-fight-night-tournamented-e-ancora-giorgio-petrosyan.htm|title=Janus Fight Night Tournament Ancora Giorgio Petrosyan|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226203916/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/756/janus-fight-night-tournamented-e-ancora-giorgio-petrosyan.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref>

In early 2008, Petrosyan made the transition to [[Kickboxing#European rules|Oriental rules]] [[kickboxing]] when he signed with the [[It's Showtime (kickboxing)|It's Showtime]] organization, making his promotional debut with a unanimous decision victory over [[Luís Reis]] at ''[[Balans: It's Showtime 75MAX Trophy Final 2008]]'' in [['s-Hertogenbosch]], Netherlands on March 15, 2008.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.shakuta.com/?p=6 |title=IT'S SHOWTIME FINALE TROPHY MAX 75 |work=shakuta.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716051127/http://www.shakuta.com/?p=6 |archive-date=2011-07-16 }}</ref> April 2008 was another pivotal month in Petrosyan's career as he made his first foray onto the [[K-1]] circuit, TKOing Mikel Colaj in five at ''[[K-1 Italy Oktagon 2008]]'' in Milan on the twelfth and forcing two second round [[standing eight count]]s en route to taking a unanimous decision over [[Chris Ngimbi]] at the ''[[K-1 World Grand Prix 2008 in Amsterdam]]'' [[supercard]] at the [[Amsterdam ArenA]] on the twenty sixth.<ref>[http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/950/k-1-gp-in-amsterdam-che-spettacolo.htm] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226203653/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/950/k-1-gp-in-amsterdam-che-spettacolo.htm|date=December 26, 2013}}</ref>

Following a brief return to the Muay Thai scene with wins over Çağrı Ermiş in [[Tilburg]], Netherlands, on May 24, 2008, and Rafik Bakkouri in [[Abano Terme]], Italy, on June 28, 2008,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/1028/abano-grand-prix-i-risultati.htm|title=Abano Grand Prix Results|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226204037/http://www.boxingbar.com/ita/1028/abano-grand-prix-i-risultati.htm|archive-date=December 26, 2013}}</ref> Petrosyan did not fight again until November when he took to the ring twice within three weeks. On November 8, he beat [[Naruepol Fairtex]] by unanimous decision at ''Janus Fight Night: The Legend''. Then, on November 29, he took another decision over [[Warren Stevelmans]] at ''[[It's Showtime 2008 Eindhoven]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.muaythaionline.org/features/bestfarangfighteraward2008.html|title=Best Farang Fighter Award 2008|access-date=December 26, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090301051905/http://muaythaionline.org/features/bestfarangfighteraward2008.html|archive-date=March 1, 2009}}</ref>

===Consecutive K-1 World MAX Tournament Championships (2009–2010)===
Petrosyan took the World Kickboxing Network (WKN) Intercontinental Welterweight (-69.9&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb) Oriental Championship on January 31, 2009, when he stopped David Javakhia with a [[Liver shot|liver kick]] inside the opening stanza in [[Turin]], Italy. On March 14, 2009, at ''[[Oktagon presents: It's Showtime 2009]]'' in Milan, he scored his first win over a truly elite fighter, taking a points victory against [[Andy Souwer]]. Using just about everything in his arsenal to keep Andy Souwer off-balance and out of his range, Petrosyan was seemingly in control of the exchanges. However, the fight got dramatic when the judges unexpectedly ruled it a draw after three rounds, sending the fight into an extension round to decide the winner. Things got even more heated when Souwer was dropped by a punch in the extension round and referee Joop Ubeda declined to give him a count. In the end, the judges saw things the way of Petrosyan and gave him the unanimous decision, in what was considered a major upset.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fightersmag.co.uk/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=176&Itemid=73|title=Sports Magazine and News}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2009/3/17/800846/it-s-showtime-milan-result|title=IT'S SHOWTIME Milan - Results and GIFs|author=smoogy|work=Bloody Elbow|date=2009-03-17}}</ref>

Following a unanimous decision win against [[Faldir Chahbari]] at ''[[It's Showtime 2009 Amsterdam]]'' on May 16, 2009, Petrosyan faced another one-time K-1 MAX champion in the form of [[Albert Kraus]] in the tournament's quarter-final stage at the ''[[K-1 World MAX 2009 World Championship Tournament Final 8]]'' in [[Tokyo]], Japan on July 13, 2009. Petrosyan defeated him soundly, winning a unanimous decision and qualifying for the round of four three months later. On October 26, 2009, at the ''[[K-1 World MAX 2009 World Championship Tournament Final]]'' in [[Yokohama]], Japan, he defeated [[Yuya Yamamoto]] in the semi-finals but suffered a broken hand in the process. In round one, he dropped Yamamoto with a left hook, but the Japanese fighter was able to return to his feet following the referee's count. He was still visibly hurt, however, and Petrosyan swarmed to knock him out with a right hook seconds later. Advancing to the final despite his hand injury, he met Andy Souwer for the second time. Petrosyan dominated all three rounds, and dropped Souwer with a knee to the body in the second, en route to a unanimous decision victory, clinching the coveted K-1 World MAX Tournament Championship.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2009/10/26/1101785/k-1-max-2009-tournament-final|title=K-1 MAX 2009 Tournament Final Results (with Videos)|author=Chris Nelson|work=Bloody Elbow|date=2009-10-26}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mmafighting.com/2009/10/26/k-1-world-max-2009-final-results|title=K-1 World Max 2009 Final Results|author=Ray Hui|work=MMA Fighting|date=2009-10-26}}</ref>

On July 18, 2011, in Tokyo at ''[[REBELS 8 & It's Showtime Japan Countdown-1]]'', Petrosyan dominated [[Hinata (kickboxer)|Hinata]] to a unanimous points victory, controlling the fight by slipping almost all of Hinata's punches, checking low kicks and catching body kicks to disrupt his opponent's rhythm. ===Glory (2012–2015)===
Petrosyan signed with the newly founded [[Glory (kickboxing)|Glory]] organization a week after the Kyshenko fight and was immediately entered into the forthcoming Glory 2012 Lightweight (-70&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb) Slam tournament.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://liverkick.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1388:giorgio-petrosyan-signs-with-glory-will-fight-in-may&catid=37:kickboxing|title=Giorgio Petrosyan Signs With Glory, Will Fight in May|author=Dave Walsh|work=liverkick.com|date=5 February 2020 }}</ref> At the Lightweight Slam's opening round at ''[[Glory 1: Stockholm]]'' on May 26, 2012, Petrosyan was drawn against Fabio Pinca in a rematch eight years in the making. Petrosyan went to work from the beginning, attacking his opponent. Pinca was able to withstand the onslaught, however, and even pressed the issue on becoming more offensive minded in the fight. As the match went on, Petrosyan used this as an advantage and methodically countered with hooks and liver kicks. Petrosyan's elusiveness was also on display as he ducked and weaved out of the way of Pinca's punches and answered with a counter strike every time before winning a unanimous decision to advance to the round of eight.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.hyefighters.com/2012/05/26/hyefighter-giorgio-petrosyan-wins-in-sweden/|title=HyeFighter Giorgio Petrosyan Wins In Sweden|work=hyefighters.com|date=27 May 2012|access-date=26 December 2013|archive-date=26 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200926152513/http://www.hyefighters.com/2012/05/26/hyefighter-giorgio-petrosyan-wins-in-sweden/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lowkickmma.com/K-1/Golden-Glory-First-16-Recap-and-Results-15990|title=Golden Glory First 16 Recap and Results|author=Mike Searson|work=LowKick MMA|date=26 May 2012}}</ref>

Moving on to ''[[Glory 3: Rome]]'' on November 3, 2012, in [[Rome]], Italy, Petrosyan went up against [[Ky Hollenbeck]] in the quarter-finals, winning by TKO when Hollenbeck suffered a torn [[anterior cruciate ligament]] in the second round after dropping the American with a perfectly timed left hook in the first. He then beat [[Davit Kiria]] by unanimous decision in the semis and, in the final, faced [[Robin van Roosmalen]] in a highly anticipated showdown. Petrosyan was able to nullify the hard-punching Dutchman by using rangy jabs and lead teep kicks to control the distance while landing power combos at will and took the unanimous points victory to win the third major tournament of his career.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.liverkick.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=2004:glory-3-rome-final-8-live-results&catid=39:europe&Itemid=59|title=GLORY 3 Rome - Final 8: Live Results|author=Dave Walsh|work=liverkick.com|date=20 September 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.muaythaiauthority.com/2012/11/results-and-recap-glory-3.html|title=Results and Recap: Glory 3 - Muay Thai Authority|work=muaythaiauthority.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226190942/http://www.muaythaiauthority.com/2012/11/results-and-recap-glory-3.html|archive-date=2013-12-26}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.liverkick.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=2006:giorgio-petrosyan-once-again-proves-hes-the-best-kickboxer-in-the-world&catid=39:europe&Itemid=59|title=Giorgio Petrosyan Once Again Proves He's The Best Kickboxer In The World|author=Dave Walsh|work=liverkick.com|date=5 February 2020 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2012/11/5/3600762/weekly-kicks-giorgio-petrosyan-glory-results-k-1-k1-max-grand-prix-2012-fight-card-superkombat|title=Weekly Kicks: Giorgio Petrosyan wins at Glory, K-1 names Grand Prix fights, Superkombat preview|author=Fraser Coffeen|work=Bloody Elbow|date=2012-11-05|access-date=2013-12-26|archive-date=2012-11-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121112012553/http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2012/11/5/3600762/weekly-kicks-giorgio-petrosyan-glory-results-k-1-k1-max-grand-prix-2012-fight-card-superkombat|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In a one-off match outside of Glory in Trieste on March 2, 2013, he turned in an uncharacteristically aggressive performance against [[Ole Laursen]] as he dropped Laursen with a knee to the liver before knocking him out with a high kick immediately after, leaving the outmatched Dane unconscious on the canvas for several minutes. This was Petrosyan's his first legitimate stoppage win in three years.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://liverkick.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=2339:giorgio-petrosyan-gets-first-ko-in-3-years-against-ole-laursen-video&catid=37:kickboxing|title=Giorgio Petrosyan Gets First KO in 3 Years Against Ole Laursen (Video)|author=Dave Walsh|work=liverkick.com|date=5 February 2020 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2013/3/4/4062538/kickboxing-video-giorgio-petrosyan-vs-ole-laursen-full-fight-video-KO|title=Kickboxing video: Giorgio Petrosyan vs. Ole Laursen full fight video with huge KO|author=Fraser Coffeen|work=Bloody Elbow|date=2013-03-04|access-date=2013-12-26|archive-date=2014-04-01|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140401051915/http://www.bloodyelbow.com/2013/3/4/4062538/kickboxing-video-giorgio-petrosyan-vs-ole-laursen-full-fight-video-KO|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Competing in the ''[[Glory 12: New York - Lightweight World Championship Tournament]]'' in [[New York City, New York]], [[United States]] on November 23, 2013, Petrosyan was knocked out for the first time by eventual champion [[Andy Ristie]] in the semi-finals. He was unable to find his rhythm due to the Surinamese fighter's pressure but nonetheless remained in control for the first two rounds of the match. In the third, however, Ristie came out aggressively, landing a right hand by way of a switch-step that had Petrosyan stunned and following up with a left uppercut, putting an end to Petrosyan's forty-two fight, six year undefeated streak.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Coffeen |first1=Fraser |title=Glory 12 results: Andy Ristie wins, Giorgio Petrosyan loses, and history is made in MSG |url=https://www.bloodyelbow.com/2013/11/24/5140902/glory-12-results-andy-ristie-upset-giorgio-petrosyan-loses-ko-spike |website=bloodyelbow.com |date=24 November 2013 |access-date=16 April 2021 |archive-date=16 April 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210416230008/https://www.bloodyelbow.com/2013/11/24/5140902/glory-12-results-andy-ristie-upset-giorgio-petrosyan-loses-ko-spike |url-status=dead }}</ref> After the fight, Petrosyan revealed that he had broken his hand early in the first round.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.glorykickboxing.com/news/biggest-upsets-of-2013:b7a32608-86fd-4247-ae5a-da7495d255f8 |title=Biggest Upsets of 2013 |date=12 July 2017 |website=[[Glory (kickboxing)|Glory]] |access-date=12 July 2017}}</ref>

Petrosyan faced Artem Pashporin for the W5 71&nbsp;kg world title on February 18, 2017, at W5 Grand Prix Kitek in Russia.<ref>{{cite press release|url=http://fightnewswire.com/artem-pashporin-vs-giorgio-petrosyan-set-w5-grand-prix-kitek/|title=Artem Pashporin vs. Giorgio Petrosyan set for W5 Grand Prix Kitek|work=fightnewswire.com|access-date=2017-01-30|archive-date=2017-02-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170202164148/http://fightnewswire.com/artem-pashporin-vs-giorgio-petrosyan-set-w5-grand-prix-kitek/|url-status=dead}}</ref> He won the fight by unanimous decision.<ref>{{cite web |title=W5 Grand Prix Kitek XXXIX Results: Petrosyan Dominates Pashporin, Stetsurenko Continues Winning Ways |url=https://combatpress.com/2017/02/w5-grand-prix-kitek-xxxix-petrosyan-vs-pashporin-results/ |website=combatpress.com |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref>

In his second bout for Bellator Kickboxing, Petrosyan faced [[Amancio Paraschiv|Amansio Paraschiv]] at [[Bellator MMA in 2017#Bellator 176|Bellator Kickboxing 5]] on April 8, 2017. He won the fight by unanimous decision.<ref>{{cite web |last1=FCF Staff |title=Bellator Kickboxing 5: Giorgio Petrosyan Outscores Amansio Paraschiv |url=http://fcfighter.com/bellator-kickboxing-5-giorgio-petrosyan-outscores-amansio-paraschiv/ |website=fcfighter.com |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref>

Petrosyan was scheduled to fight Jonay Risco for the [[ISKA]] Super Welterweight (-70&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb) K-1 Rules World Championship at ''PetrosyanMania'' in [[Monza]], Italy on October 14, 2017. However, Risco withdrew due to injury and Petrosyan instead fought [[Chris Ngimbi]] for the vacant title. Petrosyan won via unanimous decision and became the new ISKA Super Welterweight K-1 World Champion.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://iskaworldhq.com/petrosyan-mania/ |title=PETROSYAN MANIA |date=October 6, 2017 |publisher=ISKA World Headquarters |access-date=October 6, 2017 |archive-date=October 6, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171006204629/http://iskaworldhq.com/petrosyan-mania/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

On July 14, 2018, at [[Bellator MMA in 2018#Bellator 203|Bellator Kickboxing 10]] in Rome, Italy, Petrosyan defeated [[Chingiz Allazov]] by unanimous decision, scoring a knock down in the first round.<ref>{{cite news |last=Tatum |first=Rob |date=July 14, 2018 |title=Bellator Kickboxing 10 Results: Varga Dethrones Ross, Petrosyan Cruises Past Allazov |url=https://combatpress.com/2018/07/bellator-kickboxing-10-ross-vs-varga-results/ |work=Combat Press |access-date=July 14, 2018}}</ref>

===ONE Championship (since 2018)===
In April 2018, Petrosyan signed with [[ONE Championship]], unveiling their Super Series segment as a figurehead, a venture which would intertwine kickboxing and Muay Thai bouts within their MMA based events.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Bassam |first1=Tom |title=One Championship strikes out with new kickboxing league |url=https://www.sportspro.com/news/one-championship-new-kickboxing-league/ |website=SportsPro |date=12 February 2018 |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref> Petrosyan found himself in the co-main event slot for ''[[2018 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Heroes of Honor|ONE: Heroes of Honor]]'' in Manila, Philippines, squaring off against Lion Fight veteran [[Jo Nattawut]], a bout the Armenian-Italian won with his technical defensive kickboxing expertise via a unanimous decision.<ref>{{cite web |title=Giorgio Petrosyan announces ONE arrival with victory over Jo Nattawut |url=https://www.asiaone.com/asia/giorgio-petrosyan-announces-one-arrival-victory-over-jo-nattawut |website=asiaone.com |date=21 April 2018 |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref>

After securing the [[Bellator Kickboxing]] lightweight championship on a one-fight stint in July, Petrosyan returned to the Asian stage in November, for ''[[2018 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Heart of a Lion|ONE: Heart Of a Lion]]'', taking on [[Lumpinee Stadium]] Muay Thai champion [[Sorgraw Petchyindee Academy]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Legend Petrosyan Returns at One: Heart of the Lion |url=https://kickboxingz.com/legend-petrosyan-returns-at-one-heart-of-the-lion/ |website=kickboxingz.com |date=17 October 2018 |access-date=16 April 2021 |archive-date=16 April 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210416231005/https://kickboxingz.com/legend-petrosyan-returns-at-one-heart-of-the-lion/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Petrosyan returned in February 2019, competing for a second time in his brother Armen's kickboxing promotion, PetrosyanMania. He successfully defended his [[International Sport Karate Association|ISKA]] Super Welterweight (-70&nbsp;kg/154&nbsp;lb) K-1 Rules World Championship against Japanese kickboxer Atsushi Tamefusa.<ref>{{cite web |title=Giorgio Petrosyan Retains ISKA World Title With First Round Knockout |url=https://www.iskaworldhq.com/giorgio-petrosyan-retains-iska-world-title-with-first-round-knockout/ |website=iskaworldhq.com |access-date=16 April 2021 |archive-date=18 April 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210418035758/https://www.iskaworldhq.com/giorgio-petrosyan-retains-iska-world-title-with-first-round-knockout/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Petrosyan was expected to return to ONE later that year, having recently being announced as a participant in the upcoming [[2019 in ONE Championship#ONE Kickboxing Featherweight Grand Prix bracket|ONE Super Series Kickboxing Featherweight Grand Prix]]. The event was expected to start around May.<ref>{{cite web |title=Petrosyan, Yodsanklai on board for ONE Super Series Grand Prix |url=https://boec.com/petrosyan-yodsanklai-on-board-for-one-super-series-grand-prix/ |website=boec.com |date=20 February 2019 |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref>

In the Grand Prix Quarter-Finals, he faced [[Phetmorakot Petchyindee Academy]] on May 17, 2019, at ''[[2019 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Enter the Dragon|ONE Championship: Enter the Dragon]]''. Petrosyan lost by split decision but the result was reversed to a no contest due to illegal clinching and a rematch was scheduled for ''[[2019 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Masters of Destiny|ONE Championship: Masters of Destiny]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mmafighting.com/2019/5/22/18635825/one-championship-overturns-giorgio-petrosyans-loss-no-contest-petchmorrakot-petchyindee|title=ONE Championship overturns Giorgio Petrosyan's loss to no-contest|website=MMA Fighting|date=22 May 2019|access-date=2019-10-13}}</ref> On July 12, 2019, Petrosyan defeated Phetmorakot by unanimous decision to advance to the Grand Prix Semi-Finals, where he was scheduled to face [[Jo Nattawut]] for a second time.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Videos |title=Jo Nattawut says he learned from Giorgio Petrosyan loss ahead of ONE: 'Dreams of Gold' rematch (Video) |url=https://asianmma.com/jo-nattawut-says-he-learned-from-giorgio-petrosyan-loss-ahead-of-one-dreams-of-gold-rematch/ |website=asianmma.com |date=15 August 2019 |access-date=16 April 2021}}</ref> On August 16, 2019, Petrosyan defeated Jo Nattawut by KO in the first round at ''[[2019 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Dreams of Gold|ONE Championship: Dreams of Nattawut|website=www.onefc.com|date=16 August 2019|access-date=2019-10-13}}</ref> In doing so, he advanced to the ONE Super Series Kickboxing Featherweight Grand Prix Final, where he is scheduled to face [[Samy Sana]] at ''[[2019 in ONE Championship#ONE Championship: Century|ONE Championship: Century]]''.<ref>{{cite web |title=Petrosyan vs Sana: The Million Dollar Match On October 13, 2019, Petrosyan defeated Samy Sana by unanimous decision in the Grand Prix Final to become the inaugural ONE Kickboxing Featherweight World Grand Prix Champion, in addition to winning a USD$1 million cash prize.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.onefc.com/news/giorgio-petrosyan-masterclass-wins-kickboxing-world-grand-prix/|title=Giorgio Petrosyan Masterclass Wins Kickboxing World Grand Prix|website=www.onefc.com|date=13 October 2019|access-date=2019-10-13}}</ref>
