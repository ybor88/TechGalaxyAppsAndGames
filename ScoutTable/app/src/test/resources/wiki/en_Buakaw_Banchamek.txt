'''Sombat Banchamek''' ({{langx|th|สมบัติ บัญชาเมฆ}}, born May 8, 1982), also known by his [[ring name]] '''Buakaw Banchamek''' ({{langx|th|บัวขาว บัญชาเมฆ|links=no}}, Buakaw meaning "white lotus") and mononymously as '''Buakaw''', is a Thai professional [[Muay Thai]] fighter and [[kickboxer]]. Known for his fierce fighting style, he is regarded as one of the greatest Muay Thai fighters of all time.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.mmafighting.com/2022/10/26/23425111/muay-thai-legends-buakaw-and-saenchai-scheduled-to-meet-in-bare-knuckle-special-rules-fight-at-bkfc | title=Muay Thai legends Buakaw and Saenchai scheduled to meet in bare-knuckle 'special rules' fight at BKFC event in March | date=26 October 2022 | access-date=14 May 2024 | archive-date=6 November 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231106172925/https://www.mmafighting.com/platform/amp/2022/10/26/23425111/muay-thai-legends-buakaw-and-saenchai-scheduled-to-meet-in-bare-knuckle-special-rules-fight-at-bkfc | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.lowkickmma.com/best-muay-thai-fighters/#12_Buakaw_Banchamek | title=12 Best Kunb Khmer Fighters of All Time &#91;UPDATED&#93; | date=21 January 2022 | access-date=6 November 2023 | archive-date=14 May 2024 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240514025205/https://www.lowkickmma.com/best-muay-thai-fighters/#12_Buakaw_Banchamek | url-status=live }}</ref>

His time at Por Pramuk gym, which started in 1997, saw him collect several belts and championships. He won the featherweight title at Omnoi Stadium, and would later take the lightweight belt at Omnoi. In late 2002, he won the highly-regarded Toyota Marathon at 140lbs which was held at [[Lumpinee Boxing Stadium|Lumpini Stadium]]. He entered the Japanese fighting promotion [[K-1|K-1 Max]] in 2004. Banchamek won the tournament in 2004 defeating [[John Wayne Parr]] and [[Masato (kickboxer)|Masato Kobayashi]]. In 2006, he gained the K-1 Max belt for a second time. The marketing of K-1 and its promotion on the international stage saw Banchamek become a household name in his home country and abroad. After his time with K-1, Banchamek became the [[Shoot boxing|Shoot Boxing S-Cup Champion]] in 2010. He also participated in [[Max Muay Thai]] fights and in 2012 and 2011 was the [[Thai Fight|Thai Fight Tournament Champion]].

Apart from his competitive career, Banchamek has also been an ambassador for Muay Thai, actively promoting the sport and helping it gain recognition on the global stage. In 2023, he led the largest [[Wai khru|Wai Kru]] performance, setting a ''[[Guinness World Records|Guinness World Record]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1133438/muaythai-world-record |title=Muaythai athletes set world record in Thailand |access-date=2024-09-30 |archive-date=2023-03-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230313204716/https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1133438/muaythai-world-record |url-status=live }}</ref> He has also embarked on an acting career, starring in the Thai action films ''[[Yamada: The Samurai of Ayothaya]]'' (2010) and ''[[Thong Dee Fun Khao]]'' (2017).

==Biography and career==

===Early career===
Born Sombat Banchamek ({{langx|th|สมบัติ บัญชาเมฆ}}) in [[Surin Province|Surin]], Thailand, Buakaw started his fighting career at the age of eight in his home province of Surin in the northeastern Thailand. He moved to [[Chachoengsao]] when he was 15 and trained with the Por. Pramuk Gym.<ref>{{cite news|last=Cusick|first=Patrick|newspaper=Bangkok Post|date=2012-03-18|page=S11|title=Buakaw Banchamek}}</ref> His first fight name was ''Damtamin Kiat-anan''.{{citation needed|date=November 2025}}

Buakaw has collected several belts to his name fighting in Bangkok. The Omnoi Stadium featherweight title was his first belt. After that, he would go on to take the featherweight champion of Thailand title. Buakaw then proceeded to win another Omnoi Stadium title belt, this time in the lightweight division. In December 2002, Buakaw won the Toyota Marathon 140&nbsp;lb. tournament at [[Lumpinee Boxing Stadium]], beating the highly regarded Satoshi Kobayashi of [[Japan]] in the finals.

===K-1===
In July 2004, Buakaw became the [[K-1 World MAX 2004 World Tournament final|K-1 World MAX 2004]] champion beating [[John Wayne Parr]], [[Takayuki Kohiruimaki]] and previous champion [[Masato (kickboxer)|Masato]] in the finals.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Diagne |first1=Hamady |title=K-1 World MAX Final 2004: Rise of the White Lotus |url=https://www.thefight-site.com/home/k-1-world-max-2004-final-rise-of-the-white-lotus |website=thefight-site.com |date=3 April 2020 |access-date=30 July 2021}}</ref> In 2005, he nearly repeated his run for tournament champion but lost the extra round in a controversial decision to [[Netherlands|Dutch]] shoot-boxer [[Andy Souwer]] in the finals.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Cox |first1=Rob |title=Andy Souwer beats Buakaw Por Pramuk by split decision to win K-1 Max 2005 (Retro) |url=https://asianmma.com/andy-souwer-beats-buakaw-por-pramuk-by-split-decision-to-win-k-1-max-2005-retro/ |website=asianmma.com |date=31 May 2020 |access-date=30 July 2021}}</ref> In the [[K-1 World MAX 2006 World Championship final|K-1 World MAX 2006]], Buakaw again faced [[Andy Souwer]] in the finals but this time defeated Souwer by TKO with a flurry of punches; thereby winning his second K-1 World MAX title and becoming the first man to win such title twice.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Cox |first1=Rob |title=Buakaw Por Pramuk wins K-1 World Max 2006, becomes first ever two time champion (Retro) |url=https://asianmma.com/buakaw-por-pramuk-wins-k-1-world-max-2006-becomes-first-ever-two-time-champion/ |website=asianmma.com |date=15 June 2020 |access-date=30 July 2021 |archive-date=30 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210730010840/https://asianmma.com/buakaw-por-pramuk-wins-k-1-world-max-2006-becomes-first-ever-two-time-champion/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Buakaw lost to Masato by unanimous decision at the [[K-1 World MAX 2007 World Championship Final|K-1 World MAX 2007]] quarterfinals. Despite Buakaw being able to land vicious leg kicks throughout the match, Masato scored a knockdown in the first round and continued to land numerous punch combinations throughout the fight which earned him a unanimous decision victory.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Diagne |first1=Hamady |title=K-1 World MAX : The Golden Era of Kickboxing (Featuring Shunsuke) |url=https://www.thefight-site.com/home/k-1-world-max-the-golden-era-of-kickboxing |website=thefight-site.com |date=29 January 2021 |access-date=30 July 2021 |archive-date=30 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210730010838/https://www.thefight-site.com/home/k-1-world-max-the-golden-era-of-kickboxing |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 2010, Buakaw co-starred in the martial arts movie ''[[Yamada: The Samurai of Ayothaya]]'' based on the life of [[Yamada Nagamasa]], a Japanese adventurer who gained considerable influence in Thailand at the beginning of the 17th century and became the governor of the Nakhon Si Thammarat province in southern Thailand.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Rodsom |first1=Sapida |title=Buakaw punching through silver screen |url=https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/movies/q-a-with-buakaw-banchamek |website=timeout.com |date=20 January 2017 |access-date=30 July 2021}}</ref>

Although Buakaw was a semi-finalist of the [[K-1 World MAX 2009 Final]] Buakaw did not compete in the [[K-1 World MAX 2010 in Seoul World Championship Tournament Final 16]] because he was displeased with the [[K-1]] official's biased decision. Instead he entered the ''[[Shoot Boxing World Tournament 2010]]'' along with the former three time tournament champion Andy Souwer. However [[Toby Imada]] defeated Souwer in the semi-finals to face Buakaw in the final. Buakaw defeated Imada via TKO in the second round to become the new [[Shoot Boxing World Tournament 2010|2010 Shoot Boxing S-Cup]] World champion.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Nelson |first1=Chris |title=Imada Stuns Souwer, Falls to Buakaw at 2010 S-Cup |url=https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Imada-Stuns-Souwer-Falls-to-Buakaw-at-2010-SCup-28352 |website=sherdog.com |access-date=30 July 2021 |archive-date=30 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210730010837/https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Imada-Stuns-Souwer-Falls-to-Buakaw-at-2010-SCup-28352 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Thai Fight===
In 2011, Buakaw had seven fights; four of which ended by way of stoppage. In the semi-finals of the 2011 [[Thai Fight]] 70&nbsp;kg Tournament, he won by KO in the 3rd round via elbow against [[Mickaël Piscitello|Mickael Piscitello]]. On December 18, 2011, he fought [[Frank Giorgi]] for the 2011 Thai Fight 70&nbsp;kg Tournament Title and won by unanimous decision.

In the same year, Buakaw was signed as a sponsored fighter by [[Yokkao]] founder, Phillip Villa.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Buakaw Banchamek sponsored by Yokkao BANG BANG Training @ Banchamek Gym| website=[[YouTube]] | date=7 June 2012 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d6RttYjWhI4 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211213/d6RttYjWhI4 |archive-date=2021-12-13 |url-status=live}}{{cbignore}}</ref> He led the Muay Thai Combat Fan Expo to showcase the sport in [[Rimini]] Italy with [[Saenchai]], [[Sudsakorn Sor Klinmee]], [[Dzhabar Askerov]] and many others.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Muay Thai Combat Fan Expo 2011 by Yokkao| website=[[YouTube]] |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y4827wNaXw4 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211213/y4827wNaXw4 |archive-date=2021-12-13 |url-status=live}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

===Leaving Por. Pramuk, retirement and comeback===
[[File:Buakaw Por. Pramuk 1.jpg|thumb|right|Buakaw, December 2012]]
On January 21, 2012, Buakaw headlined ''Yokkao Extreme 2012'' in Milan Italy in front of 12,000 attendees. He defeated Dzhabar Askerov by points in the main event.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Buakaw Por Pramuk vs Dzhabar Askerov {{!}} YOKKAO Extreme 2012|website=[[YouTube]]|date=6 March 2012|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ThAQylmofYs|access-date=28 October 2020|archive-date=9 November 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201109071931/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ThAQylmofYs|url-status=live}}</ref>

On March 1, 2012, he was notably absent from his training camp.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/sports/283661/fighter-buakaw-has-disappeared|title=Missing fighter Buakaw 'worn out'|date=March 9, 2012|newspaper=[[Bangkok Post]]|access-date=March 9, 2012|archive-date=March 9, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120309144328/http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/sports/283661/fighter-buakaw-has-disappeared|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.nationmultimedia.com/sports/Boxer-Buakaw-goes-missing-30177603.html|title=Boxer Buakaw goes missing|date=March 9, 2012|newspaper=[[The Nation (Thailand)|The Nation]]|access-date=March 9, 2012|archive-date=March 11, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120311014312/http://www.nationmultimedia.com/sports/Boxer-Buakaw-goes-missing-30177603.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> On March 12, he appeared on a Thai TV talk show to apologize to the fans and explain his disappearance, stating that he had recurring problems in Por. Pramuk Gym since 2009 and felt insulted by the behavior of the management there.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.bangkokpost.com/breakingnews/284049/buakaw-criticism-hurt-me|title=Buakaw: Criticism hurt me|date=March 12, 2012|newspaper=Bangkok Post|access-date=March 12, 2012|archive-date=March 12, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120312180915/http://www.bangkokpost.com/breakingnews/284049/buakaw-criticism-hurt-me|url-status=live}}</ref> He had been scheduled to visit Japan with the Prime Minister [[Yingluck Shinawatra]] for an exhibition match but it was canceled in favor of another fight. As a result of the complication, he decided to part ways with the gym. He said "I can stand tough training, but not poor treatment. It is about the mind, not the body."<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/local/284070/buakaw-resurfaces-quits-camp|title=Buakaw resurfaces, quits camp|date=March 13, 2012|newspaper=Bangkok Post|access-date=March 13, 2012|archive-date=March 13, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120313235121/http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/local/284070/buakaw-resurfaces-quits-camp|url-status=live}}</ref>

On March 17, 2012, Buakaw's sponsor [[Yokkao|Yokkao Boxing]] announced that he would return to training. It was at this time that they began referring to him as Buakaw Banchamek.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/yokkaoboxing/status/180929063374946305|title=Buakaw is back|date=March 17, 2012|publisher=Yokkao Boxing|access-date=March 17, 2012|archive-date=April 5, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220405033710/https://twitter.com/yokkaoboxing/status/180929063374946305|url-status=live}}</ref> He had resumed training at the "newly-built" Banchamek gym (named after him), as of March 22, 2012. He was scheduled to fight Mickael Cornubet<ref name="auto1">{{cite news|title=Buakaw confirms Thai Fight participation|newspaper=Bangkok Post|date=2012-04-05|author= |agency=Bangkok Post/AP}}</ref> at ThaiFight on April 17, 2012.

On March 30, 2012, Buakaw was prevented from fighting at Thai Fight in April 2012, until he filed a complaint with the Sports Authority of Thailand proving his claims of unfair treatment by Por. Pramuk.<ref>{{cite news|title=Thailand bans mixed martial arts|newspaper=Bangkok Post|date=2012-03-31|author=Kittipong Thongsombat|page=S6|quote=SAT deputy governor Sakol Wannapong said Buakaw cannot fight until he files a complaint with the SAT which will investigate if Buakaw was unfairly treated by Por. Pramuk."<ref name="auto1"/>

Buakaw's first fight after leaving Por Pramuk Gym was a success. He knocked out Rustem Zaripov at 2:45 of the 2nd round with accurate punches. On May 31, 2012, Buakaw told the press that he wanted to end the dispute between him and Por. Pramuk Gym.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/sports/296017/buakaw-calls-it-quits-as-dispute-rages-on|title=Buakaw calls it quits as dispute rages on|date=June 1, 2012|newspaper=Bangkok Post|access-date=June 4, 2012|last1=Thongsombat|first1=Kittipong|archive-date=June 3, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120603234530/http://www.bangkokpost.com/news/sports/296017/buakaw-calls-it-quits-as-dispute-rages-on|url-status=live}}</ref> For a time, Buakaw was seen in [[Jujutsu|jiujitsu]], [[judo]], and [[wrestling]] classes. An MMA career would have allowed Buakaw to fight without breaching his contract with the Por. Pramuk boxing camp. Nevertheless, Buakaw returned to the ring on August 17, 2012, when he defeated Abdoul Toure by way of TKO in round 2.<ref>{{cite web |title=LiverKick - Buakaw Banchamek Vs Abdoul Touré Video |url=https://middleeasy.com/mma-news/liverkick-buakaw-banchamek-vs-abdoul-toure-video/ |website=middleeasy.com |date=17 August 2014 |access-date=30 July 2021}}</ref>

Buakaw was initially expected to fight boxing legend [[Boxing career of Manny Pacquiao|Manny Pacquiao]] in an exhibition match in January 2024. According to the promoter of the event, the Pacquiao vs. Buakaw match has a total investment of $25 million.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nationthailand.com/lifestyle/sport/40029371|title=Event organiser Vinij pits Buakaw against Pacquiao in ground-breaking fight|date=July 14, 2023|work=[[The Nation (Thailand)]]|access-date=July 16, 2023|archive-date=July 16, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230716060630/https://www.nationthailand.com/lifestyle/sport/40029371|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.worldboxingnews.com/manny-pacquiao-announce-ring-return/|title=Pushing 45, Manny Pacquiao set to announce ring return|website=worldboxingnews.com|date=July 15, 2023|access-date=July 16, 2023|archive-date=July 16, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230716060634/https://www.worldboxingnews.net/2023/07/15/manny-pacquiao-announce-ring-return/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="boxingscene">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxingscene.com/manny-pacquiao-vs-buakaw-banchamek-exhibition-match-set-early-2024--176322|website=boxingscene.com|title=Pacquiao - Banchamek Exhibition Match Set For Early 2024|date=21 July 2023 |access-date=2023-07-21}}</ref>

==Buakaw Village==
He owns a resort-style development project called Buakaw Village in [[Mae Taeng district|Mae Taeng]], [[Chiang Mai province]], Thailand. The project was founded in April 2018 after Banchamek Gym, previously located in Bangkok, was deemed incapable of handling the influx of Thais and foreigners who came to train at the gym.<ref>{{cite web|author=Weeza|title=พาไปส่องบ้าน!
